In short

What is Bobinsana?

Bobinsana (Calliandra angustifolia) is a plant of the Fabaceae (baklagiller) family. Traditions that use it: Amazon. Part used: stem, bark.

What is Bobinsana used for?

Traditional use: It is the third most used plant in the strict diets (sama) of the San Martín Quechua, appearing 21 times in 122 recorded diets. Scientific evidence: traditional. Pipecolic acid and twelve of its derivatives have been found in the leaves; some have been reported to cause muscle contraction through serotonin.

Is Bobinsana safe?

Safety label: caution. Its safety in humans has not been studied. Do not use in pregnancy, while breastfeeding or with medicines.

Traditional use

Chazuta (San Martín)

It is the third most used plant in the strict diets (sama) of the San Martín Quechua, appearing 21 times in 122 recorded diets. The diets last two to eight weeks; the plant remedy is taken while nearly fasting and in seclusion [1].

Iquitos

Mestizo healers count it among the plants that “teach medicine” when taken with a diet [2].

Yanesha and Asháninka

The Yanesha use it for tiredness in old people, stomach ache and as a purgative; the Asháninka use it to strengthen newborns, help babies walk sooner and strengthen the elderly [3].

In nature

Where it grows
Along rivers and streams in the Peruvian Amazon.
Harvest season
A perennial shrub of riverbanks with red, brush-like flowers.
Local names
Bobinsana, bobensana, remo caspi

Mix-up risk

It can be confused with other Calliandra species.

Preparation

Traditionally the stem and bark are steeped in water or liquor and taken during a diet. Home use is not recommended.

What science says

Pipecolic acid and twelve of its derivatives have been found in the leaves; some have been reported to cause muscle contraction through serotonin. In the laboratory the plant inhibited the COX-1 enzyme but did not reduce swelling in rats. There are no clinical studies in humans [1].

Safety

Caution

Its safety in humans has not been studied. Do not use in pregnancy, while breastfeeding or with medicines.
