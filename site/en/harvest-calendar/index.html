Anatolia · month by month

What to gather when: a medicinal plant calendar

The gathering season of Anatolian plants in the catalog, starting with this month. High-risk, protected and legally restricted plants are left out.

When gathering: pick only plants you can identify with certainty (see poisonous look-alikes), away from roadsides and sprayed fields; take only part of a plant so it can grow back.

November (this month)

  • Barley · Mostly sown in autumn and harvested in June; spring sowing is also practised. It ripens earlier than wheat.
  • Bearberry · Leaves are picked in late summer and autumn.
  • Bittersweet · Purple flowers from June to September, glossy red berries in autumn; the drug is gathered from the stems in spring, before the leaves.
  • Burdock · Purple flowers in hooked heads from July to September; the root is lifted in the autumn of the first year, before the plant flowers.
  • Butcher’s broom · Small greenish flowers in late winter and spring; bright red berries in autumn and winter. The root and rhizome are lifted in autumn.
  • Chicory · Sky-blue flowers from June to September, opening in the morning and closing in the afternoon. The root is lifted in autumn.
  • Chilli pepper · A summer crop; fruits ripen and redden in late summer and autumn, then are dried for storage.
  • Common polypody · Evergreen; the rhizomes are lifted in autumn and dried.
  • Couch grass · It heads from June to September. The rhizome is lifted in spring or autumn, washed and dried.
  • Cowslip · Yellow flowers from March to May; the flower is gathered then, the root in autumn.
  • Dandelion · Leaves in spring, roots in autumn.
  • Grapevine leaf · Leaves for cooking are picked in May and June while still tender. Leaves for the medicinal extract are harvested in autumn, after they have reddened.
  • Lentil · Sown in winter or spring; in Turkey red lentil is mostly sown in autumn and harvested in June and July.
  • Licorice · Roots are dug in autumn or spring.
  • Madder · Roots are dug in the autumn of the second or third year.
  • Marshmallow · Roots in autumn, flowers July–August.
  • Myrtle · Leaves in early summer, berries November–December.
  • Oak · Bark is taken from young branches in spring; oaks flower in spring and their acorns ripen in autumn.
  • Pomegranate · Fruit ripens September–November.
  • Pot marigold · Orange and yellow flowers from June into autumn; the flowers are gathered fully open and dried in shade.
  • Pumpkin seed · Flowers in summer, fruit ripens in autumn; the seeds are taken from the ripe fruit and dried.
  • Ribwort plantain · Flowers in short brown spikes on long stalks from spring to autumn; the leaves are gathered in flower.
  • Rose hip · Fruits are picked September–November, once softened by the first frosts.
  • Saffron · Flowers are hand-picked early in the morning in October–November.
  • Sea buckthorn · Berries are picked September–November, after the first frosts.
  • Shepherd’s purse · It flowers very early: in mild places it can be found in flower from late winter to autumn. The aerial parts are gathered in flower and fruit; for eating, the young leaves are gathered in spring.
  • Spiny restharrow · Pink-purple pea flowers from June to September. The root is lifted in autumn.
  • Stinging nettle · Young leaves March–May, seeds in late summer, roots in autumn.
  • Tormentil · Yellow flowers with four petals from May to September — five petals is the rule in the rose family, and four is a distinguishing feature of this species. The rhizome is lifted in autumn.
  • Tribulus · Small yellow flowers in summer; five-parted, hard-spined fruits towards autumn.
  • Valerian · Roots are dug in autumn, September–October.
  • Wheat · Winter wheat is sown in autumn and harvested in June–July.
  • Wild pansy · Three-coloured (purple-yellow-white) flowers from spring to autumn; the flowering aerial parts are gathered through summer.
