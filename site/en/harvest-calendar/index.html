Anatolia · month by month

What to gather when: a medicinal plant calendar

The gathering season of Anatolian plants in the catalog, starting with this month. High-risk, protected and legally restricted plants are left out.

When gathering: pick only plants you can identify with certainty (see poisonous look-alikes), away from roadsides and sprayed fields; take only part of a plant so it can grow back.

November (this month)

  • Bearberry · Leaves are picked in late summer and autumn.
  • Dandelion · Leaves in spring, roots in autumn.
  • Licorice · Roots are dug in autumn or spring.
  • Madder · Roots are dug in the autumn of the second or third year.
  • Marshmallow · Roots in autumn, flowers July–August.
  • Myrtle · Leaves in early summer, berries November–December.
  • Pomegranate · Fruit ripens September–November.
  • Rose hip · Fruits are picked September–November, once softened by the first frosts.
  • Saffron · Flowers are hand-picked early in the morning in October–November.
  • Sea buckthorn · Berries are picked September–November, after the first frosts.
  • Stinging nettle · Young leaves March–May, seeds in late summer, roots in autumn.
  • Valerian · Roots are dug in autumn, September–October.

About this site

A compilation made for knowledge and curiosity; it is not medical advice. Before using any plant, especially if you take medicines, are pregnant or breastfeeding, talk to a doctor or pharmacist.

Photos are from Wikimedia Commons under free licences; the author and licence are given on each plant’s page.

Evidence level

Clinical: controlled trials or an approved medicine
Limited: small or few human studies
Traditional: no or very few human studies

Safety

Generally safe in moderate use
Caution interaction or dose risks
High risk poisonous or serious side effects
