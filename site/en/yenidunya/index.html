Loquat

Eriobotrya japonica (Thunb.) Lindl.
Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

The species name of loquat is japonica, that is, "belonging to Japan"; yet according to the Kew record its native range is in China (Chongqing and Hubei). The 2016 review also describes the plant as a subtropical fruit tree native to China.

Other names
Turkish yenidünya, malta eriği; Chinese pipa (枇杷), the leaf pipaye. The Kew record gives Eriobotrya japonica as the accepted name and mentions Rhaphiolepis bibas as an alternative taxonomy. The fruit is food; the dried leaf is used in Chinese medicine. The seed releases cyanide.
Part used
The fruit (food); the dried leaves (Chinese and Japanese traditional medicine)
Region
Asia and Siberia

Use
Food, Healing
In short

What is Loquat?

Loquat (Eriobotrya japonica (Thunb.) Lindl.) is a plant of the Rosaceae (rose family) family. Traditions that use it: Asia and Siberia, Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: the fruit (food); the dried leaves (chinese and japanese traditional medicine).

What is Loquat used for?

Traditional use: The dried leaves of loquat are widely used in Chinese herbal medicine. Scientific evidence: traditional. The evidence is at the laboratory level; no registered clinical trial for loquat could be found in PubMed in this session.

Is Loquat safe?

Safety label: generally safe. The fruit is safe as food in ordinary amounts. ⚠️ The seed is not eaten. The 2020 review of amygdalin counts loquat among the members of the rose family that carry amygdalin; taken by mouth, amygdalin breaks down into hydrogen cyanide. In a zoo, two otters that ate loquat died suddenly within 4 days; cyanide was detected in the stomach contents and in fresh seeds (2002). Care is taken that small children do not swallow the fruit with its seed; if any symptom appears after seeds have been chewed and swallowed, 114 (the National Poison Information Centre in Türkiye) or 112 is called. The sources of this page hold no safety data for leaf tea and extracts in pregnancy, breastfeeding and children; it is not used in these groups. Because the leaf is traditionally regarded as lowering blood sugar, people taking diabetes medicines should consult their doctor.

Traditional use

China — pipaye, the cough leaf

The dried leaves of loquat are widely used in Chinese herbal medicine. A 2022 review (*Journal of Ethnopharmacology*) lists the traditional uses as follows: cough caused by inflammation of the lungs, shortness of breath due to asthma and cough, nausea arising from stomach disorders, restlessness and thirst. According to a 2016 review, different organs of the plant have been recorded as folk medicines in China for thousands of years.

Japan — folk medicine

The same 2022 review reports that in Japanese folk medicine the leaf is used for stomach ache, ulcers, chronic bronchitis, cancer and diabetes. These are records of traditional use; efficacy in these diseases has not been shown in humans.

Türkiye — a fruit tree of the Mediterranean coast

In Türkiye loquat is grown for its fruit. A fertiliser trial set up in 1989 at the Alata Horticultural Research Institute in the Erdemli district of Mersin followed growth, yield and quality in trees of the Akko 13 cultivar between 1993 and 1995 (the journal *Bahçe*, 1998). The study found the trial soil suitable for loquat growing.

In nature

Where it grows
According to Kew’s Plants of the World Online record its native range is China (Chongqing, Hubei); it grows as a shrub or tree. The reviews describe it as a subtropical evergreen fruit tree; a 2026 review counts frost damage among the main problems in the supply of raw material. In Türkiye it is grown on the Mediterranean coast (Mersin, Erdemli).
Harvest season
It is an evergreen fruit tree. The sources this page relies on give no flowering or harvest months.
Local names
The name yenidünya is in general use; no other local name appears in the sources of this page.

Mix-up risk

Its name is sometimes confused with medlar (*Mespilus germanica*); that is a separate species. The crude drug in Chinese medicine is the leaf; the seed cannot be used in place of the leaf, because it releases cyanide.

Preparation

The fruit is eaten fresh; the seed is removed. There is no accepted dose for the leaf; no EU, WHO or Turkish (TİTCK) monograph was found among the sources this page relies on. The crude drug used in Chinese medicine is the dried leaf. The 2022 review notes that matters such as the leaf’s bioavailability and mode of action still need to be studied. The seeds are not eaten and are not used to make tea or powder.

What science says

The evidence is at the laboratory level; no registered clinical trial for loquat could be found in PubMed in this session. The 2022 review surveyed publications from 1931 to 2022: 164 compounds, and in addition 169 volatile constituents, have been identified from the leaf, and for more than half of them no pharmacological activity has yet been reported. Triterpenes and flavonoids are held chiefly responsible for the effects; blood-sugar-lowering, anti-inflammatory and anti-tumour effects, as well as cough-suppressing, expectorant and bronchodilating effects, have been reported. The 2016 review (*International Journal of Molecular Sciences*) likewise reports that extracts act against inflammation, diabetes, cancer, bacterial infection, pain and allergy; these findings come from cell and animal models. According to the 2022 review, no side effects or signs of poisoning were observed in laboratory animals given high doses of leaf extract; this is not evidence of safety in humans.

Safety

Generally safe

The fruit is safe as food in ordinary amounts. ⚠️ The seed is not eaten. The 2020 review of amygdalin counts loquat among the members of the rose family that carry amygdalin; taken by mouth, amygdalin breaks down into hydrogen cyanide.
