Echinacea or elder? For colds

Echinacea and elder are two of the best-selling herbal products in cold season. The evidence for both is limited.

Key differences

  • Echinacea may slightly prevent or shorten colds, but results are inconsistent.
  • Elderflower is accepted for traditional use in the early symptoms of a cold; small studies with berry extract are promising but weak.
  • Raw elderberries, leaves and stems are poisonous; the berries must be cooked. Echinacea needs care in autoimmune disease and in people allergic to the daisy family.

Which one, when?

Do not expect a big effect from either. If used, start at the first symptoms and use them only briefly.

What the science says

Echinacea: According to the NCCIH it may have a small effect in preventing or shortening colds, but results are inconsistent. The EMA recognises E. purpurea aerial parts for short-term treatment of colds.

Elder: The EMA recognises elderflower for early cold symptoms as a traditional use. Small studies with berry extract reported shorter flu; the evidence is weak.

Safety

Echinacea: Can cause allergy in people allergic to the daisy family. People with autoimmune disease should be careful.

Elder: Raw berries, leaves, bark and stems contain compounds that release cyanide and cause nausea and vomiting. Always cook the berries.

Drug interactions

Elder: Diuretics (The flower’s mild diuretic effect may add up); Immunosuppressants (Theoretical interaction, as it may stimulate immunity).
