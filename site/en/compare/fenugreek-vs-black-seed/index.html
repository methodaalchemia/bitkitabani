Is fenugreek the same as black seed?

The Turkish names sound alike (çemen, çörek otu), but they are two different plants: fenugreek (Trigonella foenum-graecum) is a legume, black seed (Nigella sativa) belongs to the buttercup family. Fenugreek is the base of the spice paste on Turkish pastırma; black seed is sprinkled on pide and cheese.

Key differences

  • Fenugreek seeds are yellowish and angular, roasted and ground or soaked; black seeds are small, black and triangular, added whole or cold-pressed for oil.
  • Fenugreek is rich in fibre and saponins; some studies found lower blood sugar, but NCCIH says the evidence is not conclusive. The European Medicines Agency recognises the seed for loss of appetite. Black seed’s active compound is thymoquinone; small studies found slight drops in cholesterol, blood sugar and blood pressure.
  • Fenugreek should not be used in high doses in pregnancy and can cause allergy in people allergic to peanuts or chickpeas.
  • Large amounts of either may strengthen diabetes medicines; fenugreek may also strengthen blood thinners, and black seed oil blood-pressure medicines.

Which one, when?

In the kitchen, one flavours pastırma and the other pide. If either is taken in medicine-like amounts, people on diabetes medicines should talk to their doctor.
