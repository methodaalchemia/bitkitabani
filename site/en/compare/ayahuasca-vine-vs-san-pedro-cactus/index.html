Ayahuasca or San Pedro? Two ceremonies of the Amazon and the Andes

Ayahuasca is an Amazonian brew of a vine (Banisteriopsis caapi), usually cooked with chacruna leaves; San Pedro (Echinopsis pachanoi) is a mescaline-containing cactus of the Andes. Both are central to Indigenous healing ceremonies.

Key differences

  • The ayahuasca vine contains harmala alkaloids that block MAO-A, which makes the DMT in chacruna active when swallowed. San Pedro’s effect comes from mescaline.
  • Small clinical studies of ayahuasca in depression are promising but early; mescaline has been studied far less than psilocybin and LSD.
  • Ayahuasca can interact fatally with antidepressants and some migraine and heart medicines; deaths and abuse have been reported at unsupervised retreats.
  • San Pedro causes severe nausea, vomiting and a racing heart; it is dangerous for people with heart disease or a history of psychosis.

Which one, when?

Neither is legal in Türkiye, and neither is something to try at home. This page is for cultural and scientific information.
