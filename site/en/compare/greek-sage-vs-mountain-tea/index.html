Sage vs mountain tea: what is the difference?

In many parts of Anatolia mountain tea is also called “adaçayı” (sage), so the two are often confused. They are plants of different genera: sage is Salvia, mountain tea is Sideritis.

Key differences

  • Sage leaves are grey-green and velvety with a sharp smell. Mountain tea is dried with its flowering stems and yellowish flower spikes.
  • Because sage contains thujone, it should not be drunk for long periods or in large amounts, and is not recommended in pregnancy, breastfeeding or epilepsy. Mountain tea is generally considered safe as a tea.
  • The European Medicines Agency recognises sage for mouth and throat inflammation, and mountain tea for cough from colds and mild stomach complaints, both as traditional uses.
  • Some mountain tea species are endemic and over-harvested in the wild; cultivated plants should be preferred.

Which one, when?

A gargle for the throat is sage’s job. For a tea to drink every day through winter, thujone-free mountain tea is the better fit.

What the science says

Greek sage: The EMA recognises sage (Salvia officinalis) for mouth and throat inflammation and excessive sweating as traditional uses. The Anatolian species is rich in cineole and usually contains less thujone.

Mountain tea: The EMA recognises mountain tea as a traditional remedy for cough with colds and for mild digestive complaints; this rests on long use, not on clinical trials. Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects have been shown in the lab. A handful of small human studies, mostly on attention and cognition, give limited results.

Safety

Greek sage: Because it contains thujone, it should not be used long-term or in high doses. Not recommended in pregnancy, breastfeeding or epilepsy.

Mountain tea: Generally considered safe as a tea. For lack of data it is not recommended in pregnancy, while breastfeeding or for young children. Over-harvesting threatens some endemic species, so tea from cultivated plants is the better choice.

Drug interactions

Greek sage: Seizure medicines (antiepileptics) (Thujone can lower the seizure threshold and weaken the drug at high doses); Diabetes medicines (Can lower blood sugar; risk of hypoglycaemia with the drug); Sedatives and sleeping pills (May increase the effect).
