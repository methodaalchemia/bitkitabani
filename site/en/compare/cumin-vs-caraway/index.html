Is cumin the same as caraway?

No. Both are similar crescent-shaped seeds of the carrot family, but they taste different. Cumin (Cuminum cyminum) is the spice of meatballs; caraway (Carum carvi) the spice of rye bread and cabbage.

Key differences

  • Caraway’s strongest evidence is in combination with peppermint oil: a 2023 Cochrane review looked at the mix in functional dyspepsia. The European Medicines Agency recognises caraway for bloating and wind.
  • Cumin has small studies in irritable bowel syndrome and weight management; the evidence is weak.
  • Caraway essential oil is not taken directly by mouth; people with liver disease or gallstones should not use it.
  • Cumin is safe in spice amounts; the safety of higher, regular use is unknown, and it may lower blood sugar.

Which one, when?

For bloating, a cup of caraway tea after a meal is closer to the evidence. Do not swap one for the other in a recipe; the taste is completely different.
