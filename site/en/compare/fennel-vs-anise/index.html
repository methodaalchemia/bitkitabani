Fennel vs anise: what is the difference?

Fennel and anise are two similar-smelling seeds from the same family (Apiaceae); both owe their scent to anethole. They are often used together in teas for gas and indigestion.

Key differences

  • Fennel seeds are larger and greenish; aniseed is smaller, grey-brown and slightly hairy.
  • The European Medicines Agency recognises both for mild digestive complaints and gas; fennel also for period pain, anise also for cough from colds.
  • Because fennel contains estragole, it should not be used long-term by young children or in pregnancy. Concentrated anise oil should not be used in pregnancy or by children.
  • Both can cause allergy in people allergic to the celery and carrot family.

Which one, when?

Either is fine for a cup after a meal, and their tastes are very close. Ask a doctor before giving them to babies and young children.
