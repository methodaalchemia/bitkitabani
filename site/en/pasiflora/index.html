Passionflower

Passiflora incarnata
Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

Its Western name comes from Spanish missionaries who saw symbols of the Passion of Christ in the flower; the Turkish name çarkıfelek (“wheel of fortune”) comes from its spinning-wheel shape.

Other names
Maypop, purple passionflower, çarkıfelek
Part used
Aerial parts
Region
North America
Use
Healing, Food
In short

What is Passionflower?

Passionflower (Passiflora incarnata) is a plant of the Passifloraceae family. Traditions that use it: North America. Part used: aerial parts.

What is Passionflower used for?

Traditional use: Indigenous peoples of the south-eastern US, such as the Cherokee, ate the fruit and used the root and leaves for wounds and swellings. Scientific evidence: limited. The EMA recognises passionflower as a traditional remedy for mild stress and to help sleep.

Is Passionflower safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Generally safe; it can cause drowsiness and dizziness, so do not drive after taking it. Avoid in pregnancy. Its effect may add to alcohol, sleeping pills and sedatives.

Traditional use

North America

Indigenous peoples of the south-eastern US, such as the Cherokee, ate the fruit and used the root and leaves for wounds and swellings. Locally it is called maypop.

Europe and today

It reached Europe in the 17th century and is now used in European herbal medicines for anxiety and sleep problems.

In nature

Where it grows
Open woodland of the south-eastern US; in Türkiye passionflowers are common on fences and pergolas (mostly other species).
Harvest season
Flowering stems are cut June–August.
Local names
çarkıfelek, pasiflora

Mix-up risk

The blue-flowered Passiflora caerulea is the commonest garden species in Türkiye; the medicinal one is P. incarnata. Passion fruit comes from P. edulis.

Preparation

A teaspoon of the dried aerial parts is steeped in a cup of boiling water for 10 minutes and drunk in the evening. Standardised extract tablets also exist.

What science says

The EMA recognises passionflower as a traditional remedy for mild stress and to help sleep. A systematic review (2020) found positive results for anxiety and pre-surgery nerves but stressed that the studies were small and short. Placebo-controlled trials show a single dose before surgery reduces anxiety.

Safety

Generally safe

Generally safe; it can cause drowsiness and dizziness, so do not drive after taking it. Avoid in pregnancy. Its effect may add to alcohol, sleeping pills and sedatives.
