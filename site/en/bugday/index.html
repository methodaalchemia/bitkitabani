Wheat

Triticum aestivum
Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

Bread wheat arose from the spontaneous crossing of three different wild grasses over thousands of years, which is why its cells carry six sets of chromosomes.

Other names

Part used

In short

What is Wheat?

Wheat (Triticum aestivum) is a plant of the Poaceae (buğdaygiller) family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: grain, young shoots (wheatgrass).

What is Wheat used for?

Traditional use: Wheat was among the first cereals domesticated in the Fertile Crescent, a region that includes south-eastern Anatolia. Scientific evidence: traditional. A 2015 review of wheatgrass found promising lab and animal results, but said human studies were small with methodological problems and that larger studies are needed before it can be recommended.

Is Wheat safe?

Safety label: generally safe. People with coeliac disease, wheat allergy or gluten sensitivity should avoid wheat and wheatgrass; wheatgrass itself has no gluten but may be contaminated. Wheatgrass juice can cause nausea in some people. Wheatgrass does not replace treatment for any disease.

Traditional use

Anatolia and Mesopotamia

Wheat was among the first cereals domesticated in the Fertile Crescent, a region that includes south-eastern Anatolia. Bread, bulgur, aşure and keşkek are the foundations of the Anatolian table. In Iran and parts of Anatolia, wheat is sprouted in dishes before Nevruz; the greening wheat is a symbol of spring and abundance.

Worldwide

Wheatgrass (juice or powder of young shoots) is sold today as a “green drink” and marketed with many health claims.

In nature

Where it grows
One of the world’s most widely grown cereals; in Türkiye mainly in Central Anatolia, Thrace and the south-east.
Harvest season
Winter wheat is sown in autumn and harvested in June–July.
Local names
buğday; siyez and gernik are names of older species

Mix-up risk

Siyez of Kastamonu (Triticum monococcum) and gernik (T. dicoccum) are older, hulled wheats. Barley and rye also resemble wheat in the ear.

Preparation

For wheatgrass, seeds are soaked and sprouted in soil or a tray for 7–10 days; the shoots are cut and juiced.

What science says

A 2015 review of wheatgrass found promising lab and animal results, but said human studies were small with methodological problems and that larger studies are needed before it can be recommended. Whole-wheat products are a source of fibre. In coeliac disease, gluten in wheat damages the gut; the only treatment is a gluten-free diet.

Safety

Generally safe

People with coeliac disease, wheat allergy or gluten sensitivity should avoid wheat and wheatgrass; wheatgrass itself has no gluten but may be contaminated. Wheatgrass juice can cause nausea in some people. Wheatgrass does not replace treatment for any disease.
