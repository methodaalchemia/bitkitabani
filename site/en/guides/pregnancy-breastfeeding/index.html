Herbs to avoid or use with care in pregnancy and while breastfeeding

Herbal teas are often assumed to be harmless because they are “natural”. Yet some plants can stimulate the uterus, some contain substances that pass to the baby, and for many there are simply not enough data to show they are safe in pregnancy. Below are the plants whose catalog safety note carries a specific warning for pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Important: A plant missing from this list is not safe in pregnancy by default. In pregnancy and while breastfeeding, talk to your doctor or pharmacist before any herbal product, tea course or supplement beyond the amounts used in food.

According to the Turkish Ministry of Health phytotherapy book

The Phytotherapy Practice Book published by the Turkish Ministry of Health in 2022 gives explicit warnings for some plants in its chapters on gynaecology and pregnancy:

PlantWarning in the book
Pennyroyal (Mentha pulegium)Listed among emmenagogue herbs (herbs that bring on menstruation); most of these can cause miscarriage and are contraindicated in pregnancy.
Rue (Ruta graveolens)Listed among emmenagogue herbs; contraindicated in pregnancy.
Sage (Salvia officinalis)Listed among emmenagogue herbs; an oestrogenic effect is also reported. Contraindicated in pregnancy.
Mugwort and wormwood (Artemisia)Listed among emmenagogue herbs; contraindicated in pregnancy.
Cinnamon (Cinnamomum cassia)Listed among herbs that stimulate uterine circulation; herbs in this group are mostly contraindicated in pregnancy.
Chaste treeShould not be used in pregnancy or while breastfeeding; the book notes it may be used only under medical supervision in one specific situation in early pregnancy.
FenugreekNot suitable throughout pregnancy; not recommended while breastfeeding either, because there are not enough data.
Licorice rootAlthough used as a galactagogue in folk practice, the book states it carries risk and should not be used in pregnancy or while breastfeeding.
FlaxseedBecause there are not enough data, it should not be used in pregnancy or while breastfeeding without medical advice.
ValerianNot recommended in pregnancy or while breastfeeding without medical supervision.

The same book lists ginger both among herbs that stimulate uterine circulation and as supported by clinical data for nausea in pregnancy, so ginger should also be used after talking to a doctor. Fennel tea is reported to be usable in pregnancy and while breastfeeding at recommended doses, though some sources say for no longer than two weeks; anise tea when it does not greatly exceed amounts used in food.

Source: Turkish Ministry of Health, General Directorate of Health Services, Fitoterapi Uygulaması Kitabı (Phytotherapy Practice Book; eds. A. Tekin, İ. Tatlı Çankaya, A. U. Zeybek), Ankara, 2022; chapters on phytotherapy in gynaecology and obstetrics, and in pregnancy, the postpartum period and breastfeeding.

For nausea

The most researched plant for nausea in pregnancy is ginger; small studies are positive. Talk to your doctor before using it too. See the nausea guide →
