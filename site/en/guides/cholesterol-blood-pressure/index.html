Herbs for cholesterol and blood pressure: what does the science say?

Some foods and herbs can slightly lower cholesterol and blood pressure. These effects are small and do not replace medicines; taken alongside medicines, the effects can add up.

In short

The strongest evidence is from studies showing that eating walnuts regularly lowers LDL cholesterol, and from meta-analyses showing that garlic slightly lowers blood pressure. The effects are small and do not replace medicines.

The plants, strongest evidence first

Garlic Allium sativum

Evidence: ClinicalGenerally safe

Meta-analyses show that garlic preparations slightly lower blood pressure and cholesterol. People on blood thinners should be careful.

Flaxseed Linum usitatissimum

Evidence: ClinicalGenerally safe

Some studies saw small drops in cholesterol and blood pressure.

Flaxseed: safety, interactions and sources →

Black seed Nigella sativa

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

Small studies saw small drops in cholesterol, blood sugar and blood pressure; the studies are of low quality.

Black seed: safety, interactions and sources →

Artichoke Cynara cardunculus var. scolymus

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

Small studies saw a slight improvement in cholesterol.

Artichoke: safety, interactions and sources →

Hawthorn Crataegus monogyna

Evidence: LimitedCaution

Used traditionally for heart complaints. Because it can interact with heart medicines, do not use it without your doctor’s knowledge.

When to see a doctor: If you take blood pressure or cholesterol medicine, do not use these herbs instead of it. Ask your doctor before taking supplements; the effects can add up.
