Herbs for a sore throat

For a sore throat, herbs are usually used in two ways: as a gargle, or as teas that coat and soothe the throat. Most of these uses are recognised as traditional in Europe.

In short

The best-known gargle herb is sage. Mallow and marshmallow, which coat the throat and ease irritation, are also widely used. The effects are mild; if the pain does not pass within a few days, see a doctor.

The plants, strongest evidence first

Greek sage Salvia fruticosa

Evidence: LimitedCaution

The European Medicines Agency recognises sage for traditional use in mouth and throat inflammation. It is used as a gargle and should not be drunk for long periods or in large amounts.

Chamomile Matricaria chamomilla

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

Its use in mouth and throat inflammation is accepted in Europe. The cooled tea can also be used as a gargle.

Chamomile: safety, interactions and sources →

Common mallow Malva sylvestris

Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

Its mucilage coats the throat and soothes irritation. Traditional use in mouth and throat irritation is recognised.

Common mallow: safety, interactions and sources →

Marshmallow Althaea officinalis

Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

The root is steeped in cold water; traditional use in mouth and throat irritation is recognised.

Marshmallow: safety, interactions and sources →

Clove Syzygium aromaticum

Evidence: TraditionalCaution

Clove oil is recognised for traditional use in mouth and throat inflammation. The pure oil must not be swallowed or applied to the gums often.

Clove: safety, interactions and sources →

Mullein Verbascum thapsus ve diğer türler

Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

The flower is recognised for traditional use in throat irritation. The tea must always be strained.

When to see a doctor: See a doctor for a high fever, difficulty swallowing, shortness of breath or a sore throat lasting more than a week.
