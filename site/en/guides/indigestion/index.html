Herbs for indigestion and bloating: which ones actually work?

Gas, bloating and indigestion are among the most common reasons people drink herbal teas. Some herbs here have quite strong evidence; others rest on tradition alone. They are ranked below from the strongest evidence down.

In short

The strongest evidence is for peppermint oil: its capsules reduced irritable bowel symptoms in many trials. Ginger stands out for nausea. Chamomile, fennel, anise and artichoke are accepted in Europe for traditional use in mild indigestion.

The plants, strongest evidence first

Mint Mentha × piperita

Evidence: ClinicalGenerally safe

Peppermint oil capsules reduced irritable bowel symptoms more than placebo in many controlled trials. Peppermint leaf is accepted for traditional use in indigestion and gas. It can worsen reflux.

Ginger Zingiber officinale

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

Its use in nausea from motion sickness is accepted in Europe; small studies in pregnancy nausea are also positive.

Ginger: safety, interactions and sources →

Chamomile Matricaria chamomilla

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

Its use in mild digestive complaints is accepted in Europe.

Chamomile: safety, interactions and sources →

Fennel Foeniculum vulgare

Evidence: LimitedCaution

The seed is recognised for mild digestive complaints and gas. It should not be used for long periods by young children or in pregnancy.

Artichoke Cynara cardunculus var. scolymus

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

The leaf is recognised for traditional use in mild indigestion and bloating. People with gallstones should not use it.

Lemon balm Melissa officinalis

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

Traditional use in mild digestive complaints is recognised.

Cumin Cuminum cyminum

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

Widely used for gas and indigestion; evidence from small studies on irritable bowel is weak.

Cardamom Elettaria cardamomum

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

The belief that it aids digestion rests on traditional use.

Cardamom: safety, interactions and sources →

Anise Pimpinella anisum

Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

Traditional use in mild indigestion, bloating and gas is recognised.

Anise: safety, interactions and sources →

Yarrow Achillea millefolium

Evidence: TraditionalCaution

Recognised for traditional use in indigestion and loss of appetite; it should not be used in pregnancy.

Yarrow: safety, interactions and sources →

Rosemary Salvia rosmarinus

Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

Traditional use in mild digestive complaints is recognised.

When to see a doctor: Persistent abdominal pain, weight loss, blood in the stool, difficulty swallowing or stomach pain that wakes you at night should not be treated with herbs; see a doctor.
