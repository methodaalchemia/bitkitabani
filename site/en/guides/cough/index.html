Herbs for cough: what does the science say?

Cough is one of the oldest uses of medicinal plants. The European Medicines Agency recognises several of them as traditional herbal medicines on the basis of their long history of use. Different herbs are used for productive and dry coughs.

In short

For a productive cough the best-known herb is thyme. For a dry cough, mucilage-rich plants that coat the throat, such as mallow and marshmallow, stand out. None of them has been proven in large clinical trials.

The plants, strongest evidence first

Thyme and oregano Thymus

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

The European Medicines Agency recognises thyme as a traditional herbal medicine for productive cough. Its thymol and carvacrol show strong antimicrobial effects in the lab.

Thyme and oregano: safety, interactions and sources →

Eucalyptus Eucalyptus globulus

Evidence: LimitedCaution

Eucalyptus oil is recognised for traditional use in relieving cough from colds. Controlled studies in bronchitis have used its main compound, cineole.

Eucalyptus: safety, interactions and sources →

Licorice Glycyrrhiza glabra

Evidence: LimitedCaution

Traditional use in cough is recognised. Taken regularly and in large amounts, however, it raises blood pressure.

Licorice: safety, interactions and sources →

Common mallow Malva sylvestris

Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

The mucilage in its leaves coats the throat. Traditional use in dry cough and throat irritation is recognised.

Common mallow: safety, interactions and sources →

Marshmallow Althaea officinalis

Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

The root is very rich in mucilage. Traditional use in dry cough and mouth and throat irritation is recognised.

Marshmallow: safety, interactions and sources →

Mullein Verbascum thapsus ve diğer türler

Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

The flower is recognised for traditional use in cough and throat irritation. The tea must be strained; the hairs irritate the throat.

Mullein: safety, interactions and sources →

Anise Pimpinella anisum

Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

Traditional use in cough from colds is recognised. Its main compound, anethole, shows expectorant effects in the lab.

Anise: safety, interactions and sources →

Mountain tea Sideritis türleri

Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

Recognised for traditional use in cough from colds; there are few human studies.

Mountain tea: safety, interactions and sources →

When to see a doctor: See a doctor promptly for a cough lasting more than three weeks, a high fever, shortness of breath or blood in the phlegm. Essential oils should not be applied to the faces of babies and young children.

Limited: small or few human studies
Traditional: no or very few human studies
