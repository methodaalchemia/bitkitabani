Herbs for anxiety and stress: what has evidence and what to watch for

Some herbs used for anxiety and stress have surprisingly strong evidence. But they can interact with medicines; St John’s wort in particular weakens many drugs.

In short

The strongest evidence for anxiety is for lavender oil capsules, and for mild to moderate depression it is for St John’s wort. But St John’s wort interacts with many medicines; talk to a doctor before using it.

The plants, strongest evidence first

Lavender Lavandula angustifolia

Evidence: ClinicalGenerally safe

Standardised oral lavender oil capsules beat placebo for anxiety disorder in several controlled trials.

Lavender: safety, interactions and sources →

St John’s wort Hypericum perforatum

Evidence: ClinicalCaution

According to a Cochrane review, standardised extracts beat placebo in mild to moderate depression. But it weakens many medicines, including the contraceptive pill and blood thinners.

St John’s wort: safety, interactions and sources →

Lemon balm Melissa officinalis

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

Traditional use in mild stress is recognised; small studies report improvements in anxiety.

Lemon balm: safety, interactions and sources →

Valerian Valeriana officinalis

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

Recognised in Europe for mild nervous tension; results are mixed.

Valerian: safety, interactions and sources →

Chamomile Matricaria chamomilla

Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

Small studies on anxiety are positive.

Chamomile: safety, interactions and sources →

Ashwagandha Withania somnifera

Evidence: LimitedCaution

Small, short trials report lower stress scores. Liver injury cases have been reported; it should not be used in pregnancy.

Ashwagandha: safety, interactions and sources →

When to see a doctor: If anxiety or depression affects daily life, see a professional. If you take antidepressants or other regular medicines, do not use these herbs without asking your doctor.
Not medical advice. This page brings together entries from the catalog and summarises the science; it is not a diagnosis or treatment recommendation. Before using any plant, especially if you take medicines, are pregnant, breastfeeding or have a health condition, talk to your doctor or pharmacist.
