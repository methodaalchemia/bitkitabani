In short

What is Yarrow?

Yarrow (Achillea millefolium) is a plant of the Asteraceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, North America. Part used: flowering aerial parts.

What is Yarrow used for?

Traditional use: Crushed and applied to cuts to stop bleeding; brewed for the stomach and period pain. Scientific evidence: traditional. The EMA recognises yarrow for loss of appetite, indigestion, period pain and small wounds as traditional uses.

Is Yarrow safe?

Safety label: caution. Can cause allergy in people allergic to the daisy family. Not for use in pregnancy.

Traditional use

Anatolia

Crushed and applied to cuts to stop bleeding; brewed for the stomach and period pain.

North America

Many Indigenous peoples use the leaf for wounds, toothache and fever.

In nature

Where it grows
Meadows, roadsides and mountain pastures throughout Türkiye.
Harvest season
Picked in flower, June–August.
Local names
civanperçemi, barsama otu, binbir yaprak otu, akbaşlı

Mix-up risk

Its white umbrella flowers can recall deadly poison hemlock. Yarrow leaves are finely divided like feathers and the plant is hairy.

Preparation

Flowering stems are steeped; fresh leaves are crushed onto wounds.

What science says

The EMA recognises yarrow for loss of appetite, indigestion, period pain and small wounds as traditional uses. Human studies are few.

Safety

Caution

Can cause allergy in people allergic to the daisy family. Not for use in pregnancy.
