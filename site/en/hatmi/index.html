Marshmallow

Althaea officinalis
Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

The English name “marshmallow” comes from the plant being a relative of the mallow that grows at the edge of marshes.

In short

What is Marshmallow?

Marshmallow (Althaea officinalis) is a plant of the Malvaceae (mallow family) family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: root, leaves, flowers.

What is Marshmallow used for?

Traditional use: The root and flowers are soaked in cold water and drunk for dry cough and throat and stomach irritation. Scientific evidence: traditional. The root is very rich in mucilage, which coats the throat and stomach.

Is Marshmallow safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Generally safe. The mucilage can delay absorption of other medicines; leave an hour between them. The EMA monograph does not recommend use under 3, and sets a daily limit of 15 g of root. The TİTCK monograph writes that it is not recommended in pregnancy or while breastfeeding, that it should be stopped two weeks before surgery, and that it interacts with medicines that reduce blood clotting.

Traditional use

Anatolia

The root and flowers are soaked in cold water and drunk for dry cough and throat and stomach irritation.

Europe

In the 19th century the mucilage from the root was whipped with sugar to make the first marshmallow sweets.

In nature

Where it grows
Damp and salty soils, stream and lake banks throughout Türkiye.
Harvest season
The roots are gathered in autumn, the flowers in July–August.
Local names
hatmi, hatmi çiçeği, gülhatmi (for Alcea species)

Mix-up risk

Hollyhock (Alcea rosea) is also sold as hatmi and used similarly.

According to Baytop, although medicinal marshmallow grows in Anatolia, its flower is rarely found in trade; what herbalists sell as “marshmallow flower” is mostly hollyhock (Alcea) and other related species.

Preparation

The root is left in cold water and drunk as soon as it is prepared; it is not heated. The EMA monograph gives an adult 0.5–3 g of root in 150 ml of water several times a day and sets a daily upper limit of 15 g; for ages 3–5, 0.5–1 g, and for ages 6–11, 0.5–1.5 g, 3 times a day. It is not recommended under 3.

What science says

The root is very rich in mucilage, which coats the throat and stomach. The EMA recognises marshmallow root for mouth and throat irritation and dry cough as traditional uses.

Safety

Generally safe

Generally safe. The mucilage can delay absorption of other medicines; leave an hour between them. The EMA monograph does not recommend use under 3, and sets a daily limit of 15 g of root. The TİTCK monograph writes that it is not recommended in pregnancy or while breastfeeding, that it should be stopped two weeks before surgery, and that it interacts with medicines that reduce blood clotting.
