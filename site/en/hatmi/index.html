What is Marshmallow?

Marshmallow (Althaea officinalis) is a plant of the Malvaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: root, leaves, flowers.

What is Marshmallow used for?

Traditional use: The root and flowers are soaked in cold water and drunk for dry cough and throat and stomach irritation. Scientific evidence: traditional. The root is very rich in mucilage, which coats the throat and stomach.

Is Marshmallow safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Generally safe. The mucilage can delay absorption of other medicines; leave an hour between them.

Traditional use

Anatolia

The root and flowers are soaked in cold water and drunk for dry cough and throat and stomach irritation.

Europe

In the 19th century the mucilage from the root was whipped with sugar to make the first marshmallow sweets.

In nature

Where it grows
Damp and salty soils, stream and lake banks throughout Türkiye.
Harvest season
Roots in autumn, flowers July–August.
Local names
hatmi, hatmi çiçeği, gülhatmi (for Alcea species)

Mix-up risk

Hollyhock (Alcea rosea) is also sold as hatmi and used similarly.

Preparation

The root is soaked in cold water for a few hours; not heated.

What science says

The root is very rich in mucilage, which coats the throat and stomach. The EMA recognises marshmallow root for mouth and throat irritation and dry cough as traditional uses.

Safety

Generally safe

Generally safe. The mucilage can delay absorption of other medicines; leave an hour between them.
