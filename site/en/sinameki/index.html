In short

What is Senna?

Senna (Senna alexandrina) is a plant of the Fabaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: leaves, pods.

What is Senna used for?

Traditional use: Since Ottoman times one of the best-selling laxatives in herbal shops; its Turkish name means “senna of Mecca”. Scientific evidence: clinical. The EMA recognises senna leaf and pod for short-term treatment of occasional constipation as a well-established use.

Is Senna safe?

Safety label: caution. Do not use for more than a week; long use causes a lazy bowel and potassium loss. Using it for weight loss is dangerous: it only removes water and salts. Not for children under 12, in pregnancy or with bowel obstruction.

Traditional use

Anatolia

Since Ottoman times one of the best-selling laxatives in herbal shops; its Turkish name means “senna of Mecca”. In recent years it has also been mixed into slimming teas.

Medieval Islamic medicine

Medieval Arab and Persian physicians used it as a laxative; it spread through the Islamic world along the Hajj route.

In nature

Where it grows
Dry regions of Egypt, Sudan and India; imported into Türkiye.
Harvest season
Leaves are picked before and after flowering, pods when ripe.
Local names
sinameki, sinamekki, sene

Mix-up risk

May be mixed with other laxative leaves in herbal shops; choose reliable, labelled products.

Preparation

Leaves or pods are soaked in cold or warm water; used short-term.

What science says

The EMA recognises senna leaf and pod for short-term treatment of occasional constipation as a well-established use. Its active compounds are sennosides.

Safety

Caution

Do not use for more than a week; long use causes a lazy bowel and potassium loss. Using it for weight loss is dangerous: it only removes water and salts. Not for children under 12, in pregnancy or with bowel obstruction.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Diuretics, corticosteroids, licoriceIncrease potassium loss.
Digoxin and heart rhythm drugsLow potassium raises the risk of toxicity.
Oral medicinesFaster bowel transit may reduce absorption.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying Senna.
