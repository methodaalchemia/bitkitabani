What is Pau d’arco?

Pau d’arco (Handroanthus impetiginosus) is a plant of the Bignoniaceae (trumpet-creeper family) family. Traditions that use it: Amazon. Part used: inner bark.

What is Pau d’arco used for?

Traditional use: The inner bark is boiled for fever, infections and skin disease. Scientific evidence: traditional. Contains lapachol.

Is Pau d’arco safe?

Safety label: caution. High doses can cause nausea, vomiting and bleeding. Do not use with blood thinners.

Traditional use

Amazon and South America

The inner bark is boiled for fever, infections and skin disease.

Today

It has been marketed with claims of treating cancer; these are unproven.

In nature

Where it grows
Dry and humid forests of South America, from the Amazon to Argentina.
Harvest season
Bark is collected all year.
Local names
No Turkish folk name. Local names: pau d’arco, tahibo, lapacho

Mix-up risk

Some bark on sale comes from other species or from the outer bark.

Preparation

The inner bark of the tree (lapacho) is boiled; the outer bark and wood are not used, as the compounds considered active are in the inner bark. There is no accepted dose, and the contents of products on the market do not match one another — in some analyses the expected component was not found at all. In large amounts it can cause nausea, vomiting and bleeding.

What science says

Contains lapachol. A 1970s cancer study was stopped because effective doses caused toxic side effects.

Safety

Caution

High doses can cause nausea, vomiting and bleeding. Do not use with blood thinners.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Blood thinners and antiplatelet drugsLapachol counteracts vitamin K; risk of bleeding.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying Pau d’arco.

Conservation

CITES
Appendix II (since 2022)
Note
Handroanthus species were listed in CITES Appendix II in 2022 because of illegal logging for their valuable wood.
