Vine leaves stuffed with rice (yaprak sarma)

The Aegean and Mediterranean classic: vine leaves rolled around spiced rice and cooked in olive oil.

Prep
40 min
Total
1 h 30 min
Makes
Serves 4–5
Read this first
Amount
Safe as food
Caution
Medicinal red vine leaf extract is not the same as this dish; sudden swelling, pain or hardening in the leg needs a doctor
Do not use
People on a low-salt diet should rinse brined leaves thoroughly

Ingredients

  • 250 g brined or fresh vine leaves
  • 1 cup of rice
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • ½ cup of olive oil
  • 1 tablespoon each of currants and pine nuts
  • 1 dessertspoon dried mint, ½ dessertspoon black pepper, ½ dessertspoon allspice, 1 teaspoon sugar, salt
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • 2 cups of hot water

Method

  1. Rinse brined leaves several times to remove the salt; blanch fresh leaves in boiling water for 1–2 minutes.
  2. Soften the onion in the olive oil, add the pine nuts, then the washed rice, currants and spices and stir for 5 minutes.
  3. Put a dessertspoon of filling on the veined side of each leaf, roll tightly and pack into a pot.
  4. Add the lemon juice and hot water, weigh down with an upturned plate and cook over low heat for 40–45 minutes; serve cold.

Brined leaves are salty; if not rinsed well the dish will be too salty.

Who should be careful? Not for anyone hypersensitive to the active substance. The Turkish monograph separately lists the situations that require medical advice during use: inflammation of the skin, thrombophlebitis or subcutaneous induration, severe pain, ulceration, sudden swelling of one or both legs, and cardiac or renal failure. These are the signs that chronic venous insufficiency should not be managed with a herbal product alone: sudden swelling of a leg can indicate a clot. Preparations applied to the skin must not be used on damaged skin, around the eyes or on mucous membranes. Durations of use are limited by indication: four weeks for leg symptoms, one week for haemorrhoids and capillary fragility. The culinary use of vine leaves is not the subject of these warnings.
