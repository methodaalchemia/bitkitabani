What is Chaste tree?

Chaste tree (Vitex agnus-castus) is a plant of the Lamiaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: fruit.

What is Chaste tree used for?

Traditional use: Grows along Aegean and Mediterranean streams. Scientific evidence: clinical. Standardised fruit extract reduced premenstrual syndrome (PMS) symptoms versus placebo in several controlled trials.

Is Chaste tree safe?

Safety label: caution. It has hormonal effects and can interact with birth control, hormone therapy and dopamine-related drugs. Not for use in pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Traditional use

Anatolia

Grows along Aegean and Mediterranean streams. Its twigs are used for basketry; the fruit is brewed for irregular periods.

Ancient Greece

At the Thesmophoria festival for Demeter, women made beds of chaste-tree branches; it was a symbol of chastity.

In nature

Where it grows
Stream beds and coastal dunes in the Aegean and Mediterranean.
Harvest season
Fruit is picked September–October.
Local names
hayıt, ayıt, beşparmak otu, rahip biberi

Mix-up risk

Easy to recognise by its purple flower spikes and hand-like, five-part leaves.

Preparation

Usually taken as a standardised extract.

What science says

Standardised fruit extract reduced premenstrual syndrome (PMS) symptoms versus placebo in several controlled trials. The EMA classes this use as “well established”.

Safety

Caution

It has hormonal effects and can interact with birth control, hormone therapy and dopamine-related drugs. Not for use in pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Birth control pills and hormone therapyActs on hormone balance; may change the effect.
Dopamine-acting drugs (Parkinson’s drugs, some antipsychotics)May interact, as it acts through dopamine.
IVF medicinesMay affect ovulation treatment.
