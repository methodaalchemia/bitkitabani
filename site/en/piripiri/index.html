In short

What is Piri piri?

Piri piri (Cyperus spp.) is a plant of the Cyperaceae (sazgiller) family. Traditions that use it: Amazon. Part used: tuberous rhizome.

What is Piri piri used for?

Traditional use: Each clump of piri piri grown beside the house has its own name and healing use; treatment is usually by rubbing on the crushed rhizome. Scientific evidence: traditional. Cultivated piri piri plants host a fungus, Balansia cyperi, that produces ergot alkaloids, which narrow blood vessels and can cause uterine contractions.

Is Piri piri safe?

Safety label: caution. Its ergot alkaloids can cause uterine contractions, so it should not be used in pregnancy. There are no safety data on taking it by mouth.

Traditional use

Shipibo-Konibo (Ucayali)

Each clump of piri piri grown beside the house has its own name and healing use; treatment is usually by rubbing on the crushed rhizome. One kind protects children and treats fright (susto), nightmares, paleness and loss of appetite. Wild kinds are used in love magic, rubbed into the hair with huito to make it grow, or in fishing magic [1].

Asháninka (Tambo River)

The Asháninka distinguish 86 named kinds within just four botanical species. The rhizome juice is used for childbirth, child care, fertility, hunting, protection from sorcery and farming. Names are often kept secret and plants are passed on within families [2][3].

In nature

Where it grows
In the gardens of Indigenous communities in the western Amazon; most cultivated plants are sterile and propagated from cuttings [1].
Harvest season
A perennial sedge grown in clumps beside houses.
Local names
Piri piri, huaste, ibenki

Mix-up risk

It can be confused with Isoetes, Hesperoxiphion and Peperomia species sold under the same name in markets [4].

Preparation

Traditionally the crushed rhizome is rubbed on or its juice is used. Home use is not recommended.

What science says

Cultivated piri piri plants host a fungus, Balansia cyperi, that produces ergot alkaloids, which narrow blood vessels and can cause uterine contractions. The researchers note that some uses fit this effect but the fungus alone does not explain them all [2]. Because the plants are propagated only from cuttings, the fungus is passed on with cultivation [1].

Safety

Caution

Its ergot alkaloids can cause uterine contractions, so it should not be used in pregnancy. There are no safety data on taking it by mouth.
