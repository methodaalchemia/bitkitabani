In short

What is Quinoa?

Quinoa (Chenopodium quinoa) is a plant of the Amaranthaceae family. Traditions that use it: Andes. Part used: seeds.

What is Quinoa used for?

Traditional use: The Incas called quinoa the “mother grain” (chisaya mama); the emperor is said to have sown the first seeds of the season with a golden spade. Scientific evidence: traditional. A gluten-free seed containing all essential amino acids.

Is Quinoa safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Safe as food. If not rinsed well, the saponins can upset the stomach.

Traditional use

Andes

The Incas called quinoa the “mother grain” (chisaya mama); the emperor is said to have sown the first seeds of the season with a golden spade.

Andes today

A staple in soups, breads and drinks around Lake Titicaca.

In nature

Where it grows
The plateaus of the Andes at 2,500–4,000 metres; Peru and Bolivia.
Harvest season
Seeds are harvested April–May.
Local names
No Turkish folk name. Local names: quinua, chisaya mama

Mix-up risk

Relatives such as fat hen (C. album) look similar.

Preparation

Seeds are rinsed well to remove bitter saponins, then boiled.

What science says

A gluten-free seed containing all essential amino acids. Human studies of medicinal claims are limited.

Safety

Generally safe

Safe as food. If not rinsed well, the saponins can upset the stomach.
