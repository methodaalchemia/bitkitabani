Rosemary

In short

What is Rosemary?

Rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus) is a plant of the Lamiaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Andes. Part used: leaves, flowering sprigs.

What is Rosemary used for?

Traditional use: A hedge and garden plant on the Mediterranean coast. Scientific evidence: traditional. The EMA recognises rosemary for mild digestive complaints and, externally, for muscle and joint pain as traditional uses.

Is Rosemary safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Safe in culinary amounts. High-dose oil is not recommended in epilepsy or pregnancy.

Traditional use

Anatolia

A hedge and garden plant on the Mediterranean coast. Its oil is rubbed in for hair loss and sore muscles; its tea is drunk for digestion.

Andes

In Peru, romero is used in limpia incense and in bath water to drive away bad energy.

In nature

Where it grows
Maquis and garden hedges on the Mediterranean and Aegean coasts.
Harvest season
Can be picked all year; the aroma is strongest just before flowering.
Local names
biberiye, kuşdili, akpüren, hasalban

Mix-up risk

Can be confused with lavender leaves. Rosemary leaves are darker green, stiff and needle-like.

Preparation

Brewed as tea; the oil is diluted and rubbed in.

What science says

The EMA recognises rosemary for mild digestive complaints and, externally, for muscle and joint pain as traditional uses. There are small studies on memory; results are inconclusive.

Common mistakes

  • Putting the essential oil on the skin undiluted or drinking it.
  • High doses in people with epilepsy.
  • Therapeutic doses in pregnancy (amounts in food are fine).

Frequently asked

How do I use rosemary oil on hair?

Dilute a few drops in a carrier oil such as olive oil and apply to the scalp. Test on a small patch first for allergy.

Can I drink rosemary tea every day?

Moderate amounts are considered safe; people on blood pressure or diabetes medicine should ask their doctor.

Does rosemary improve memory?

There are interesting but small studies; no clear effect has been shown.

Safety

Generally safe

Safe in culinary amounts. High-dose oil is not recommended in epilepsy or pregnancy.
