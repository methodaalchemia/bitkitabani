In short

What is Olive leaf?

Olive leaf (Olea europaea) is a plant of the Oleaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: leaves, fruit, oil.

What is Olive leaf used for?

Traditional use: The leaves are boiled and drunk for blood pressure and blood sugar; olive oil is dripped on wounds, burns and aching ears. Scientific evidence: limited. The leaf is rich in a phenol called oleuropein.

Is Olive leaf safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Generally safe. Its effect may add to blood pressure and diabetes medicines.

Traditional use

Anatolia

The leaves are boiled and drunk for blood pressure and blood sugar; olive oil is dripped on wounds, burns and aching ears. The olive branch is a symbol of plenty and peace.

Ancient Mediterranean

The sacred tree of Athens; Olympic winners were crowned with wild olive.

In nature

Where it grows
Aegean, Mediterranean and Marmara coasts; Kilis and Hatay in the southeast.
Harvest season
Leaves all year; olive harvest October–January.
Local names
zeytin, delice (wild olive), zeytin yaprağı

Mix-up risk

Wild olive (delice) and grafted olive are the same species. Use leaves from unsprayed trees for tea.

Preparation

Dried leaves are boiled for 10–15 minutes; standardised leaf extract is also used.

What science says

The leaf is rich in a phenol called oleuropein. In a few controlled trials, leaf extract gave small drops in mildly high blood pressure. The EMA recognises olive leaf for mild systolic hypertension as a traditional use.

How strong is the evidence?

Claims often repeated online about Olive leaf, and what science says today.

Common mistakes

  • Lowering blood pressure too far with olive leaf tea while on blood pressure medicine.
  • Assuming olive leaf tea is as strong as olive leaf extract.

Frequently asked

How do I make olive leaf tea?

Boil the dried leaves for 10–15 minutes; it tastes bitter.

Does olive leaf lower blood pressure?

Some studies saw a slight drop. Tell your doctor if you take blood pressure medicine.

Can I take olive leaf extract every day?

Studies used it for a few months; long-term safety is not well known.

Safety

Generally safe

Generally safe. Its effect may add to blood pressure and diabetes medicines.
