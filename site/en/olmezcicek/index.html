Sandy everlasting

Helichrysum arenarium (L.) Moench
Evidence: TraditionalCaution

The plant’s name comes from the way the flower keeps its shape and yellow colour for years once dried: "ölmez çiçek" (undying flower) in Turkish, "everlasting" and "immortelle" in English, and Helichrysum in Latin — from the Greek for sun and gold. All four say the same two things: golden, and unfading. When a plant’s name describes its appearance this consistently rather than its use, you can see what folk naming chooses to foreground.

Other names
The Turkish monograph gives the names "ölmez çiçek, altın otu" and lists no synonyms. In English: sandy everlasting, immortelle. The name comes from the way the flower keeps its shape and colour for years once dried.

In short

What is Sandy everlasting?

Sandy everlasting (Helichrysum arenarium (L.) Moench) is a plant of the Asteraceae (daisy family) family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Europe. Part used: the flower.

What is Sandy everlasting used for?

Traditional use: The EMA monograph gives one indication, under "traditional use": digestive disorders with a feeling of fullness and bloating. Scientific evidence: traditional. As with common centaury, there is no clinical trial in humans.

Is Sandy everlasting safe?

Safety label: caution. Not used in obstruction of the bile duct, gallstones, liver disease or cholangitis. The reason is direct: the plant may stimulate bile secretion, and stimulating it in these conditions makes matters worse. Allergic reactions can develop in people sensitive to plants of the Asteraceae family — a large family, so people allergic to chamomile, chrysanthemum or sunflower should take care. Not recommended under 18, nor in pregnancy or lactation. If symptoms do not resolve over two weeks, or if adverse effects appear, consult a doctor. The Turkish monograph requires supervision by a doctor or pharmacist trained in herbal treatment. Digestive complaints should not be managed with a herbal product for long without finding out their cause.

Traditional use

Europe — fullness and bloating

The EMA monograph gives one indication, under "traditional use": digestive disorders with a feeling of fullness and bloating. The monograph also states the forms: herbal tea, infusion, decoction or macerate.

Turkey — official list

The Turkish monograph gives the part used as "the flowers" and sets the key limit: not used in obstruction of the bile duct, gallstones, liver disease or cholangitis. The reason is written in the EU monograph: the plant may stimulate bile secretion.

Anatolia

Sandy everlasting grows on dry sandy slopes in Turkey; because the yellow flower heads keep their shape after drying, they are used in dried arrangements. The same property gave the plant its name.

In nature

Where it grows
Sandy, dry, sunny slopes and pine-forest clearings of Europe and western Asia. Found in Turkey.
Harvest season
Small yellow flower heads from June to August; gathered before fully open and dried.
Local names
Ölmez çiçek, altın otu — both appear in the Turkish monograph.

Mix-up risk

It is confused with other species of the same genus: Helichrysum italicum, which grows around the Mediterranean, is sold in cosmetics and as an essential oil and is a different species; the EU monograph covers H. arenarium only. Many Helichrysum species are gathered under the names "ölmez çiçek" and "altın otu" in Turkey.

Preparation

The monograph describes tea, infusion, decoction and macerate prepared from the flowers. If symptoms do not resolve over two weeks, consult a doctor. Not used under 18.

What science says

As with common centaury, there is no clinical trial in humans. Every recent publication on the plant is laboratory work: antioxidant and anti-ageing activity measurements, chemical profiling, cell-line experiments, toxicity studies in plant models. These show what is in the plant and what it does at cell level; they do not show whether it relieves bloating in a person. The EU monograph rests the indication on long use. The one mechanism the monograph gives is stimulation of bile secretion, which is consistent with the indication: bile helps digest fats, and a feeling of fullness usually follows a fatty meal. The same mechanism explains the contraindications: if bile flow is already obstructed, or there are gallstones, stimulating secretion makes the problem worse. The evidence level is Traditional.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Blood pressure medicinesThe Turkish monograph reports one interaction, with antihypertensives. The EU monograph lists nothing, so this warning exists only in the Turkish text. Anyone on blood pressure medication should tell their doctor.
No interactions reported in the EMA monographThe European monograph lists nothing; the Turkish one reports one group.
Medicines acting on the bile ductsThere is no interaction study; the issue comes from the mechanism. The plant is used to stimulate bile secretion, so anyone on medication for gallstones or biliary disease should ask their doctor.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying Sandy everlasting.
