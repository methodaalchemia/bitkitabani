In short

What is Garlic?

Garlic (Allium sativum) is a plant of the Amaryllidaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Andes. Part used: bulb (cloves).

What is Garlic used for?

Traditional use: Hung over doors and cradles against the evil eye and evil spirits. Scientific evidence: clinical. Crushing forms allicin.

Is Garlic safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Safe as food. High doses may strengthen blood thinners; stop before surgery.

Traditional use

Anatolia

Hung over doors and cradles against the evil eye and evil spirits. Eaten raw for colds and blood pressure.

Andes

In Peru and Bolivia, ajo is used in limpia rites and carried against the evil eye; eaten for colds.

In nature

Where it grows
A crop plant; known for production in Kastamonu (Taşköprü) and Gaziantep.
Harvest season
Lifted in June–July when the leaves yellow.
Local names
sarımsak, sarmusak, sarmısak

Mix-up risk

Can be confused with wild garlic species and ornamental onions; poisonings have occurred when autumn crocus or lily-of-the-valley leaves were mistaken for wild garlic.

Preparation

Eaten raw and crushed; letting it rest 10 minutes after crushing increases the active compound.

What science says

Crushing forms allicin. Meta-analyses show garlic preparations lower blood pressure and cholesterol slightly. The NCCIH notes that these effects are small.

How strong is the evidence?

Claims often repeated online about Garlic, and what science says today.

ClaimEvidenceNote
Lowers blood pressure slightlyClinical evidenceSmall but significant drop in meta-analyses.
Lowers cholesterolLimited evidenceThe effect is small and inconsistent between studies.
Prevents coldsLimited evidenceBased on a single small study; Cochrane found the evidence insufficient.
Prevents cancerNo evidenceObservational studies exist but show no cause and effect.

Common mistakes

  • Taking high-dose garlic supplements while on blood thinners or before surgery.
  • Raw garlic stored in oil: at room temperature it risks botulism; keep it in the fridge.
  • Binding garlic to the skin for long to treat a wound: it causes chemical burns.

Frequently asked

Is eating garlic on an empty stomach beneficial?

There is no specific evidence for this; it causes heartburn in some people.

How much does garlic lower blood pressure?

Studies usually saw small drops of a few points; it is no substitute for blood pressure medicine.

Can I use garlic with HIV medicines?

Garlic supplements lower the levels of some HIV drugs; do not combine them.

Safe as food. High doses may strengthen blood thinners; stop before surgery.
