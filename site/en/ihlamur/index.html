In short

What is Linden?

Linden (Tilia tomentosa) is a plant of the Malvaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: flowers with their bract.

What is Linden used for?

Traditional use: The tea of winter evenings, drunk to sweat out a cold and to relax. Scientific evidence: traditional. The EMA recognises linden flower for cold symptoms and mild mental stress as traditional uses.

Is Linden safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Generally safe.

Traditional use

Anatolia

The tea of winter evenings, drunk to sweat out a cold and to relax. Common as a street tree in Istanbul; the flowers are gathered in June.

In nature

Where it grows
Black Sea and Marmara forests; park and street tree in cities.
Harvest season
Flowers are picked in June and early July when fully open.
Local names
ıhlamur, ıhlamur çiçeği, gümüşi ıhlamur

Mix-up risk

Some linden on sale comes from weakly scented species or old harvests. The best is yellowish flowers picked with their bract.

Preparation

Dried flowers are steeped for 5–10 minutes; not boiled.

What science says

The EMA recognises linden flower for cold symptoms and mild mental stress as traditional uses. It contains flavonoids and mucilage; clinical studies are few.

How strong is the evidence?

Claims often repeated online about Linden, and what science says today.

ClaimEvidenceNote
Eases cold symptomsTraditional onlyRecognised by the EMA as a traditional use; controlled studies are almost absent.
Relaxes and helps sleepTraditional onlyRecognised by the EMA for mild mental stress as a traditional use; not shown in studies.
Strengthens immunityNo evidenceNo human studies show this.
Helps weight lossNo evidenceNo evidence; apart from filling you up as a hot drink, no known effect.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the flowers: the volatile compounds escape and the tea turns bitter. Pour on boiled water and steep covered.
  • Linden blends sold with leaves and twigs: the valuable part is the flower and the pale green bract carrying it.
  • Relying on linden instead of seeing a doctor for a persistent fever.

Frequently asked

Can I drink linden tea every day?

There are no known serious side effects at moderate amounts (2–3 cups a day). People with heart disease or on regular medication should ask their doctor.

Can I drink linden tea in pregnancy?

The EMA does not recommend it in pregnancy or breastfeeding because of insufficient data. Ask a doctor.

Can children drink it?

The EMA defines the traditional use for children over 4; ask a doctor for younger children.
