What is Lemon balm?

Lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) is a plant of the Lamiaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: leaves.

What is Lemon balm used for?

Traditional use: Beekeepers rub hives with it to attract swarms; its Turkish name, oğul otu (“swarm herb”), comes from this. Scientific evidence: limited. The EMA recognises it for mild stress, sleep problems and mild digestive complaints as traditional uses.

Is Lemon balm safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Generally safe. People taking thyroid medicine should be careful.

Traditional use

Anatolia

Beekeepers rub hives with it to attract swarms; its Turkish name, oğul otu (“swarm herb”), comes from this. The tea is drunk to calm the nerves and for sleep.

In nature

Where it grows
Damp, half-shaded places and gardens on the Black Sea and Mediterranean coasts.
Harvest season
Picked before flowering, June–August.
Local names
melisa, oğul otu, limon otu, kovan otu

Mix-up risk

Its leaves look like mint and smell of lemon; it can be confused with other lemon-scented herbs.

Preparation

Fresh or dried leaves are steeped covered; the essential oil escapes easily.

What science says

The EMA recognises it for mild stress, sleep problems and mild digestive complaints as traditional uses. Small clinical trials report improvements in anxiety and sleep.

How strong is the evidence?

Claims often repeated online about Lemon balm, and what science says today.

Prevents Alzheimer’sNo evidenceNo human studies show this.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling it for a long time: the essential oil escapes. Steep covered.
  • Regular high doses while taking thyroid medicine (it may affect the thyroid).
  • Taking it with sleeping pills: drowsiness may increase.

Frequently asked

Does lemon balm tea help sleep?

It has a mild relaxing effect; its effect on sleep has mostly been studied with valerian.

Can I drink it every day?

Moderate amounts are considered safe; people with thyroid disease should ask a doctor.

Is oğul otu the same as lemon balm?

Yes, both are names for Melissa officinalis.

Safety

Generally safe

Generally safe. People taking thyroid medicine should be careful.
