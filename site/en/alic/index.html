In short

What is Hawthorn?

Hawthorn (Crataegus monogyna) is a plant of the Rosaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: flowering twigs, fruit.

What is Hawthorn used for?

Traditional use: The fruit is eaten fresh in autumn or made into molasses. Scientific evidence: limited. The EMA recognises hawthorn leaf and flower for temporary, nervous heart complaints as traditional uses.

Is Hawthorn safe?

Safety label: caution. Can interact with heart medicines such as digoxin, beta blockers and nitrates. Should not be used for heart disease without a doctor’s knowledge.

Traditional use

Anatolia

The fruit is eaten fresh in autumn or made into molasses. Known as a “heart herb”; the flowering twigs are brewed for palpitations and blood pressure.

In nature

Where it grows
Forest edges, scrub and hedges throughout Türkiye.
Harvest season
Flowers April–May, fruit September–October.
Local names
alıç, yemişen, akdiken, geyik elması

Mix-up risk

Firethorn (Pyracantha) berries look similar. A hawthorn fruit has one or more hard stones inside.

Preparation

Flowering twigs and leaves are steeped.

What science says

The EMA recognises hawthorn leaf and flower for temporary, nervous heart complaints as traditional uses. Small gains in exercise capacity have been seen in mild heart failure, but a large trial (SPICE) found no significant difference in deaths.

Safety

Caution

Can interact with heart medicines such as digoxin, beta blockers and nitrates. Should not be used for heart disease without a doctor’s knowledge.

Drug interactions
