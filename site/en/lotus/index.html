Sacred lotus

Nelumbo nucifera
Evidence: TraditionalGenerally safe

Lotus seeds about 1,300 years old found in a dry lake bed in China have been germinated, among the longest-lived seeds known. Its water-repelling leaves gave their name to the “lotus effect”.

Other names
Indian lotus, padma, lian
Part used
Rhizome, seed, leaf, flower
Region
Asia and Siberia
Use
Ritual and shamanic, Food, Healing
In short

What is Sacred lotus?

Sacred lotus (Nelumbo nucifera) is a plant of the Nelumbonaceae family. Traditions that use it: Asia and Siberia. Part used: rhizome, seed, leaf, flower.

What is Sacred lotus used for?

Traditional use: Its flower, rising spotless from mud, symbolises purity and enlightenment; the Buddha and Hindu gods are often shown on a lotus. Scientific evidence: traditional. Lotus leaf and seed lower blood fats and sugar and act as antioxidants in lab and animal studies; alkaloids such as neferine in the seed sprout are widely researched.

Is Sacred lotus safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Safe as food. The rhizome should not be eaten raw, as a water plant it may carry parasites. Safety data for high doses of leaf and sprout extracts are scarce; avoid in pregnancy.

Traditional use

Hinduism and Buddhism

Its flower, rising spotless from mud, symbolises purity and enlightenment; the Buddha and Hindu gods are often shown on a lotus. It is India’s national flower.

Chinese and Japanese cooking

The rhizome is sliced and fried or added to soup; the seeds go into sweets and mooncakes. Rice is wrapped in its leaves, and flower and leaf teas are drunk.

In nature

Where it grows
Shallow lakes and marshes of Asia, in still warm water; grown in some ornamental ponds in Türkiye.
Harvest season
Flowers July–August; rhizomes are dug from the mud in autumn and winter.
Local names
lotus

Mix-up risk

The water lilies of Turkish lakes (Nymphaea and Nuphar) are a different family; water-lily leaves lie flat on the water, while lotus leaves and flowers rise above it. Egypt’s sacred “blue lotus” is actually a water lily (Nymphaea caerulea).

Preparation

The rhizome is boiled, fried or pickled. The bitter green sprout is removed from the seeds, which are eaten; the sprout is made into a separate tea.

What science says

Lotus leaf and seed lower blood fats and sugar and act as antioxidants in lab and animal studies; alkaloids such as neferine in the seed sprout are widely researched. Controlled studies in people are very few. Its use as a “fat burner” in slimming teas is not evidence-based.

Safety

Generally safe

Safe as food. The rhizome should not be eaten raw, as a water plant it may carry parasites. Safety data for high doses of leaf and sprout extracts are scarce; avoid in pregnancy.
