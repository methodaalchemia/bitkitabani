In short

What is Licorice?

Licorice (Glycyrrhiza glabra) is a plant of the Fabaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Asia and Siberia. Part used: root.

What is Licorice used for?

Traditional use: In the southeast, licorice sherbet is drunk during Ramadan as a cooling, stomach-friendly drink. Scientific evidence: limited. It contains glycyrrhizin, 50 times sweeter than sugar.

Is Licorice safe?

Safety label: caution. Regular, heavy use raises blood pressure, lowers potassium and can cause heart rhythm problems. People with high blood pressure, heart or kidney disease and pregnant women should avoid it.

Traditional use

Anatolia

In the southeast, licorice sherbet is drunk during Ramadan as a cooling, stomach-friendly drink. The root is chewed for coughs and heartburn.

Asia

One of the most used plants in Chinese medicine, added to formulas to “harmonise” the other herbs.

In nature

Where it grows
River banks and wet lowland fields in Southeastern Anatolia and the Aegean; mostly wild-harvested.
Harvest season
Roots are dug in autumn or spring.
Local names
meyan, meyan kökü, biyan, piyan

Mix-up risk

Recognised by its sweet taste and yellow inside. Both its effects and its risks come from glycyrrhizin.

Preparation

The root is soaked or boiled in water.

What science says

It contains glycyrrhizin, 50 times sweeter than sugar. The EMA recognises it for coughs and mild stomach complaints as traditional uses. A glycyrrhizin-free form (DGL) has been studied for stomach ulcers.

Common mistakes

  • Drinking lots of licorice sherbet every day in summer: cases of high blood pressure and low potassium have been reported.
  • Drinking it with high blood pressure or in pregnancy.
  • Not realising licorice sweets carry the same risk.

Frequently asked

Does licorice sherbet raise blood pressure?

Yes, when drunk regularly and in quantity. People with high blood pressure should avoid it.

How much licorice is safe a day?

In Europe, glycyrrhizin intake under 100 mg a day is advised. Sherbet content varies and is hard to measure; not drinking it regularly is safest.

Can I drink licorice root in pregnancy?

Not recommended; effects on preterm birth and the baby’s development have been reported.

Safety

Caution

Regular, heavy use raises blood pressure, lowers potassium and can cause heart rhythm problems. People with high blood pressure, heart or kidney disease and pregnant women should avoid it.

Conservation

Note
Roots are mostly dug from the wild; overharvesting is shrinking populations in some areas.
