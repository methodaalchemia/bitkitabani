In short

What is Licorice?

Licorice (Glycyrrhiza glabra) is a plant of the Fabaceae (legume family) family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Asia and Siberia. Part used: root.

What is Licorice used for?

Traditional use: In the southeast, licorice sherbet is drunk during Ramadan; it is considered cooling and kind to the stomach. Scientific evidence: limited. It contains glycyrrhizin, 50 times sweeter than sugar.

Is Licorice safe?

Safety label: caution. Regular, heavy use raises blood pressure, lowers potassium and can cause heart rhythm problems. People with high blood pressure, heart or kidney disease and pregnant women should avoid it. The EMA monograph does not recommend use under 18, in pregnancy or while breastfeeding. For digestive complaints it gives 1.5–2 g of chopped root in 150 ml of boiling water, 2–4 cups a day after meals (3–8 g a day), for no more than four weeks. The TİTCK monograph does not recommend use in hypertension, kidney disorders, liver disease, cardiovascular disorders or low blood potassium.

Traditional use

Anatolia

In the southeast, licorice sherbet is drunk during Ramadan; it is considered cooling and kind to the stomach. The root is chewed against cough and heartburn.

Asia

It is one of the most frequently used plants in Chinese medicine; it is added to prescriptions because it “brings the other herbs into harmony”.

In nature

Where it grows
River banks and wet lowland fields in Southeastern Anatolia and the Aegean; mostly wild-harvested.
Harvest season
The roots are dug up in autumn or in spring.
Local names
meyan, meyan kökü, biyan, piyan

Mix-up risk

Recognised by its sweet taste and yellow inside. Both its effects and its risks come from glycyrrhizin.

According to Baytop, the roots of bitter licorice (Glycyrrhiza echinata) are not valued because of their bitter compounds, but they are gathered and sold mixed with true licorice root.

Preparation

The root is soaked or boiled in water. The amount the EMA monograph gives for digestive complaints: 1.5–2 g of chopped root in 150 ml of boiling water, 2–4 cups a day after meals (3–8 g a day), not exceeding four weeks; if symptoms last more than two weeks, see a doctor. For cough, 1.5–4.5 g per dose, 2–3 times a day; if it lasts more than a week, see a doctor. The monograph does not recommend use under 18, in pregnancy or while breastfeeding.

What science says

It contains glycyrrhizin, 50 times sweeter than sugar. The EMA recognises it for coughs and mild stomach complaints as traditional uses. A glycyrrhizin-free form (DGL) has been studied for stomach ulcers.

Common mistakes

  • Drinking lots of licorice sherbet every day in summer: cases of high blood pressure and low potassium have been reported.
  • Drinking it with high blood pressure or in pregnancy.
  • Not realising licorice sweets carry the same risk.

Frequently asked

Does licorice sherbet raise blood pressure?

Yes, when drunk regularly and in quantity. People with high blood pressure should avoid it.

How much licorice is safe a day?

In Europe, glycyrrhizin intake under 100 mg a day is advised. Sherbet content varies and is hard to measure; not drinking it regularly is safest.

Can I drink licorice root in pregnancy?

Not recommended; effects on preterm birth and the baby’s development have been reported.

Note
