Silver birch

Betula pendula
Evidence: ClinicalGenerally safe

In Central Asian Turkic “kayın” means birch; in Anatolia the name passed to a different tree, the beech (Fagus). Birch bark owes its whiteness to a compound called betulin.

In short

What is Silver birch?

Silver birch (Betula pendula) is a plant of the Betulaceae (huşgiller) family. Traditions that use it: Asia and Siberia, Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Europe. Part used: leaf, bark, sap.

What is Silver birch used for?

Traditional use: Birch is a sacred tree among the Altai and Siberian peoples. Scientific evidence: clinical. The EMA recognises birch leaf for traditional use to increase urine flow and flush the urinary tract in minor urinary complaints.

Is Silver birch safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Leaf tea is generally safe; it rarely causes stomach upset or allergy. Do not use it for oedema caused by heart or kidney disease or where fluid intake must be limited. The EMA does not recommend it under 12; data in pregnancy and breastfeeding are lacking. Birch pollen is a major cause of spring hay fever, and people allergic to it may react to some fruits.

Traditional use

Siberia and Central Asia

Birch is a sacred tree among the Altai and Siberian peoples. The frame of the kam’s drum is made of birch or cedar; accounts of the Altai kam’s journey to the sky describe him climbing a notched birch pole.

Northern and eastern Europe

In Russia and the Baltic countries birch sap tapped from the trunk in spring is drunk fresh. Leaf tea is used to increase urine flow, and bundles of birch twigs are used to beat the body in the sauna.

In nature

Where it grows
Temperate and cold forests from Europe to Siberia; native in Türkiye in the high forests of north-eastern Anatolia and planted in cities as an ornamental.
Harvest season
Leaves are picked in spring while young and pale green; sap is tapped in March–April before the buds burst.
Local names
huş, akkayın

Mix-up risk

Anatolian beech (Fagus orientalis) is a different tree with a smooth grey trunk; birch has a white trunk that peels like paper.

Preparation

One or two dessertspoons of dried leaves are steeped in a cup of boiling water for 10 minutes; drunk several times a day with plenty of water.

What science says

The EMA recognises birch leaf for traditional use to increase urine flow and flush the urinary tract in minor urinary complaints. A gel containing betulin from the bark (Oleogel-S10) sped up healing of burn wounds in a controlled trial, and in a 223-patient trial in epidermolysis bullosa, a rare genetic skin disease, it closed more wounds.

Safety

Generally safe

Leaf tea is generally safe; it rarely causes stomach upset or allergy. Do not use it for oedema caused by heart or kidney disease or where fluid intake must be limited. The EMA does not recommend it under 12; data in pregnancy and breastfeeding are lacking. Birch pollen is a major cause of spring hay fever, and people allergic to it may react to some fruits.
