In short

What is Fennel?

Fennel (Foeniculum vulgare) is a plant of the Apiaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: seeds, bulb.

What is Fennel used for?

Traditional use: The tea is given for colic in babies and to boost milk in nursing mothers. Scientific evidence: limited. The EMA recognises fennel seed for mild digestive complaints, gas and period pain.

Is Fennel safe?

Safety label: caution. Because it contains estragole, the EMA advises against long-term use in young children and pregnancy. People allergic to celery or carrot may react.

Traditional use

Anatolia

The tea is given for colic in babies and to boost milk in nursing mothers. In the Aegean, the bulb and stalks go into dishes.

In nature

Where it grows
Roadsides, fallow land and coasts in the Aegean and Mediterranean.
Harvest season
Leaves in spring, seeds August–September.
Local names
rezene, arapsaçı, raziyane, malatura

Mix-up risk

The same family includes deadly poison hemlock (Conium maculatum). Fennel has a clear aniseed smell; never pick umbrella-flowered plants that lack it.

Preparation

Seeds are lightly crushed and steeped.

What science says

The EMA recognises fennel seed for mild digestive complaints, gas and period pain. There are small studies on infant colic.

Common mistakes

  • Giving babies fennel tea regularly: the EMA advises against it because of estragole.
  • Overlooking the sugar in instant “baby tea” granules.
  • Thinking fennel and anise are the same plant.

Frequently asked

Does fennel tea cause or relieve gas?

It is traditionally used to relieve gas.

Can babies have fennel tea?

The European Medicines Agency does not recommend it under 4. Ask a paediatrician about colic.

Does fennel increase milk?

A common belief, but there is no reliable evidence.

Safety

Caution

Because it contains estragole, the EMA advises against long-term use in young children and pregnancy. People allergic to celery or carrot may react.
