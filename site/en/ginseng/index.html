In short

What is Ginseng?

Ginseng (Panax ginseng) is a plant of the Araliaceae family. Traditions that use it: Asia and Siberia. Part used: root.

What is Ginseng used for?

Traditional use: Used in China and Korea for 2,000 years for strength, long life and old age; human-shaped roots are most prized. Scientific evidence: limited. Contains ginsenosides.

Is Ginseng safe?

Safety label: caution. Can cause insomnia. May interact with blood thinners and diabetes medicines.

Traditional use

East Asia

Used in China and Korea for 2,000 years for strength, long life and old age; human-shaped roots are most prized.

Siberia

Hunters searching for wild ginseng in the Ussuri region prayed where they found a root and marked it with red thread.

In nature

Where it grows
Forests of Korea, northeastern China and the Russian Far East; mostly cultivated today.
Harvest season
Roots are dug in autumn from plants at least 4–6 years old.
Local names
ginseng, insan kökü

Mix-up risk

American ginseng (P. quinquefolius) and Siberian ginseng (Eleutherococcus) are different plants.

Preparation

The root is dried, or steamed to make “red ginseng”; used as tea or extract.

What science says

Contains ginsenosides. Some studies found small effects on blood sugar and immunity; the NCCIH says the evidence is limited.

Safety

Caution

Can cause insomnia. May interact with blood thinners and diabetes medicines.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Blood thinners (warfarin)May weaken warfarin.
Diabetes medicines and insulinLowers blood sugar.
MAO inhibitorsInsomnia, headache and restlessness have been reported.
Stimulants and caffeineStimulant effects may add up.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying Ginseng.

Conservation

Note
Overharvesting has made wild Panax ginseng very rare; it is protected in Russia. Almost all roots in trade are cultivated.
Alternative
Cultivated ginseng.

Culture bridge

Human-shaped roots. East Asia’s ginseng and the Mediterranean’s mandrake were seen as magical and sacred because their roots look human. Read the comparison →
