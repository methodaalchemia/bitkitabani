In short

What is Carob?

Carob (Ceratonia siliqua) is a plant of the Fabaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: pods, seeds.

What is Carob used for?

Traditional use: On the Mediterranean coast the pods are made into molasses, eaten by the spoon for coughs and anaemia. Scientific evidence: limited. Rich in fibre and natural sugars.

Is Carob safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Generally safe. The molasses is high in sugar; people with diabetes should eat it in moderation.

Traditional use

Anatolia

On the Mediterranean coast the pods are made into molasses, eaten by the spoon for coughs and anaemia. Carob flour was given to children with diarrhoea.

Ancient Mediterranean

Jewellers used its seeds as weights because they were believed to be very uniform; the word “carat” comes from here.

In nature

Where it grows
Mediterranean and Aegean coasts; common in Antalya, Mersin and Muğla.
Harvest season
Pods are picked August–September when they turn brown.
Local names
keçiboynuzu, harnup, harup, buynuz

Mix-up risk

No look-alike; only ripe, dried pods are eaten.

Preparation

Pods are dried and eaten, ground into flour or boiled down into molasses.

What science says

Rich in fibre and natural sugars. Small studies report that carob fibre slightly lowers cholesterol and speeds recovery from diarrhoea in children. Locust bean gum from the seed (E410) is used as a food thickener.

Safety

Generally safe

Generally safe. The molasses is high in sugar; people with diabetes should eat it in moderation.
