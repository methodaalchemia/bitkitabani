What is Andiroba?

Andiroba (Carapa guianensis) is a plant of the Meliaceae family. Traditions that use it: Amazon. Part used: seed oil, bark.

What is Andiroba used for?

Traditional use: The bitter oil from the seeds is applied to insect bites, joint pain and wounds and used as a mosquito repellent. Scientific evidence: traditional. Contains limonoids.

Is Andiroba safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Generally safe on the skin. Not to be swallowed.

Traditional use

Amazon

The bitter oil from the seeds is applied to insect bites, joint pain and wounds and used as a mosquito repellent.

Amazon

The oil is mixed with wax to make mosquito-repellent candles.

In nature

Where it grows
Flooded and humid forests of the Amazon basin.
Harvest season
Seeds are collected when they fall in the rainy season.
Local names
No Turkish folk name. Local names: andiroba, tangare

Mix-up risk

Several Carapa species are used under the same name.

Preparation

Seeds are boiled and left for a few weeks, then the oil is drained.

What science says

Contains limonoids. Insect-repellent and anti-inflammatory effects have been seen in the lab; human studies are few.

Safety

Generally safe

Generally safe on the skin. Not to be swallowed.
