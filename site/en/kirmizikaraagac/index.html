What is Slippery elm?

Slippery elm (Ulmus rubra) is a plant of the Ulmaceae family. Traditions that use it: North America. Part used: inner bark.

What is Slippery elm used for?

Traditional use: Many Indigenous peoples use the inner bark for sore throats, coughs, wounds and burns; in times of famine bark porridge was food. Scientific evidence: traditional. The inner bark is rich in mucilage that turns jelly-like in water and coats the throat and stomach.

Is Slippery elm safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Generally safe. The mucilage can delay absorption of medicines; take it an hour or two apart.

Traditional use

North America

Many Indigenous peoples use the inner bark for sore throats, coughs, wounds and burns; in times of famine bark porridge was food.

Today

Used in throat lozenges and stomach teas.

In nature

Where it grows
Forests of the eastern US and southern Canada.
Harvest season
Bark is peeled in spring when the sap runs.
Local names
No established Turkish name; kırmızı karaağaç, kaygan karaağaç

Mix-up risk

Commercial powders may contain other elm species.

Preparation

Inner bark powder is mixed with water into a paste or drunk.

What science says

The inner bark is rich in mucilage that turns jelly-like in water and coats the throat and stomach. The US FDA accepts slippery elm as an over-the-counter throat soother; other uses are little studied.

Safety

Generally safe

Generally safe. The mucilage can delay absorption of medicines; take it an hour or two apart.

Conservation

Note
A tree stripped of its bark dies; together with Dutch elm disease, this has reduced wild populations.
Alternative
Marshmallow root and mallow contain similar mucilage.
