What is Ajo sacha (garlic vine)?

Ajo sacha (garlic vine) (Mansoa alliacea) is a plant of the Bignoniaceae (katalpagiller) family. Traditions that use it: Amazon. Part used: leaf, stem, root.

What is Ajo sacha (garlic vine) used for?

Traditional use: The Yanesha use it for diarrhoea with stomach pain, fever, flu, rheumatic pain, skin ulcers and boils; the Asháninka use it for “mal aire” (bad air) [1]. Scientific evidence: traditional. It smells of garlic when crushed.

Is Ajo sacha (garlic vine) safe?

Safety label: caution. Its safety in humans has not been studied. Do not use in pregnancy, while breastfeeding or in children.

Traditional use

Peruvian Amazon

The Yanesha use it for diarrhoea with stomach pain, fever, flu, rheumatic pain, skin ulcers and boils; the Asháninka use it for “mal aire” (bad air) [1].

Iquitos

Mestizo healers count it among the “teacher plants” that can be taken alone [2]. A healer Luna worked with gives ajo sacha baths above all against “saladera”, a kind of bad luck, and rheumatism; with the proper diet this plant too is believed to “teach medicine” [5].

Chazuta (San Martín)

It is one of the plants most often used in the strict diets (sama) of the San Martín Quechua, which last several weeks [6].

Ecuador

The national herbal pharmacopoeia of Ecuador records traditional uses for headache, abdominal, rheumatic and muscle pain, fever, cough, fatigue and colds; it is also used as a repellent [3].

In nature

Where it grows
Amazon forests and village gardens.
Harvest season
A perennial vine with purple flowers.
Local names
Ajo sacha, sacha ajo, ajo de monte

Mix-up risk

It can be confused with Cordia alliodora, sold under the same name in northern Peruvian markets [4].

Preparation

Traditionally the leaves and stems are boiled or used in baths. Internal use at home is not recommended.

What science says

It smells of garlic when crushed. Laboratory studies report sulphur compounds, sterols, iridoids and naphthoquinones; there are no clinical studies in humans [3].

Safety

Caution

Its safety in humans has not been studied. Do not use in pregnancy, while breastfeeding or in children.
