What is Yacón?

Yacón (Smallanthus sonchifolius) is a plant of the Asteraceae (daisy family) family. Traditions that use it: Andes. Part used: root tuber.

What is Yacón used for?

Traditional use: The sweet, juicy tuber is eaten raw like fruit and quenches thirst on journeys. Scientific evidence: limited. Rich in fructooligosaccharides (FOS), fibres that raise blood sugar little.

Is Yacón safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Safe as food. Too much causes gas and bloating. The long-term safety of leaf tea is unknown.

Traditional use

Andes

The sweet, juicy tuber is eaten raw like fruit and quenches thirst on journeys. The leaves are brewed in folk use for blood sugar.

Andes

Depictions of yacón appear on Moche and Nazca ceramics.

In nature

Where it grows
Andean valleys at 1,000–3,500 m in Peru, Bolivia and Ecuador.
Harvest season
Tubers are harvested 6–7 months after planting.
Local names
No Turkish folk name. Local names: yacón, llaqon, aricoma

Mix-up risk

Related to Jerusalem artichoke (Helianthus tuberosus) and looks similar.

Preparation

The tuber is peeled and eaten raw; its juice is boiled down to a thick syrup. Its sweetness comes from fructo-oligosaccharides, which are not digested in the gut: too much causes wind, bloating and diarrhoea, so start with a small amount. The leaf tea is a separate thing — its long-term safety is unknown, and this page gives no amount for the leaf.

What science says

Rich in fructooligosaccharides (FOS), fibres that raise blood sugar little. Small studies showed benefit for constipation.

Safety

Generally safe

Safe as food. Too much causes gas and bloating. The long-term safety of leaf tea is unknown.
