In short

What is Cinnamon?

Cinnamon (Cinnamomum verum) is a plant of the Lauraceae family. Traditions that use it: Asia and Siberia. Part used: inner bark.

What is Cinnamon used for?

Traditional use: Ceylon cinnamon is a traditional medicine of Sri Lanka and cassia of China; both are considered warming and good for digestion. Scientific evidence: limited. There are many small studies on blood sugar; results are inconsistent, and the NCCIH says there is not enough evidence that cinnamon helps any condition.

Is Cinnamon safe?

Safety label: caution. Safe in food amounts. Cassia, the most common supermarket cinnamon, is high in coumarin and can harm the liver if eaten by the teaspoon every day. Ceylon cinnamon has very little coumarin.

Traditional use

Sri Lanka and China

Ceylon cinnamon is a traditional medicine of Sri Lanka and cassia of China; both are considered warming and good for digestion.

Anatolia

Essential for sahlep, aşure, rice pudding and winter teas; Ottoman cooks also used it in meat dishes.

In nature

Where it grows
Sri Lanka, southern India, China and Indonesia; does not grow in Türkiye.
Harvest season
Bark is peeled in the rainy season when the sap runs.
Local names
tarçın, darçın

Mix-up risk

Ceylon cinnamon is thin, many-layered and brittle; cassia is thick, single-layered and hard. Powders are hard to tell apart.

Preparation

Bark sticks are boiled or the powder added to food.

What science says

There are many small studies on blood sugar; results are inconsistent, and the NCCIH says there is not enough evidence that cinnamon helps any condition.

Safety

Caution

Safe in food amounts. Cassia, the most common supermarket cinnamon, is high in coumarin and can harm the liver if eaten by the teaspoon every day. Ceylon cinnamon has very little coumarin.
