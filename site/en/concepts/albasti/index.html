Albastı (alkarısı)

The evil being believed to attack women who have just given birth and their newborns, and the onion, garlic, iron and red ribbon used against it.

The concept’s plantBlack seedNigella sativaBlack seeds were found in Tutankhamun’s tomb.Plant page →

In Anatolia the being called alkarısı, al, alanası or albası is believed to attack mainly women who have just given birth and their babies. The illness it causes is called albastı or albasması; today it is known to have been puerperal fever, an infection after childbirth [1].

The protections are similar across a wide area: the mother is not left alone for 40 days and a lamp is kept burning; a Qur’an, a mirror, an onion, garlic or an amulet is placed nearby, and iron such as a knife, scissors or tongs, or bread and a needle, under the pillow; a red ribbon is tied to the mother’s head and to the cradle. After an attack a horse was brought into the room to neigh, and barley was put on the patient for the horse to eat [1]. Field material from Sivas adds burning Black seed at the mother’s head and placing onion, Garlic, a knife and a needle by the bed [2].

Among the Kyrgyz, albarstı can attack men as well as women; the specialists who drive it away are called kuuçu or kuuğunçu. Because the eagle was thought to be the only animal that can see it, an eagle was brought into the house during a difficult birth, and eagle claws and wolf teeth were hung on cradles. The Gagauz put embers at the door; Turkmen put a knife, salt and bread under the pillow [3].

Whoever catches an alkarısı with a needle or pitch is believed to become ocaklı, and objects belonging to such people protect against it; the belief has Central Asian roots [3][4]. This is a record of belief: fever, shivering or foul-smelling discharge after childbirth is a medical emergency.
