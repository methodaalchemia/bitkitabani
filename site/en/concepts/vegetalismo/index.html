Concept of the day

Vegetalismo

The tradition of mestizo healers in the Peruvian Amazon who are believed to learn from plants.

The concept’s plantChiric sanangoBrunfelsia grandifloraIts flowers open purple, then turn lavender and white; in English it is called “yesterday, today and tomorrow”.Plant page →

Mestizo healers who work with plants around Iquitos are called “vegetalistas”, plant specialists. The name sets them apart from “oracionistas”, who work only with prayer, and “espiritistas”, who work only with spirits. Among vegetalistas there are specialisms: the “purguero” who uses ayahuasca, the “tabaquero” who works mainly with tobacco, the “camalonguero” who uses camalonga seeds, the “perfumero” who works with flower essences, and others [1].

Asked where their knowledge came from, all four healers Luna interviewed gave the same answer: “La purga misma te enseña”, “the purge [ayahuasca] itself teaches you”. For them many plants, perhaps all, have a “mother” or spirit; these spirits teach diagnosis, which plant to use and how, the use of tobacco smoke, and the icaros [1].

The “teacher plants” include Mapacho tobacco, Toé, Chuchuhuasi, Abuta, Chiric sanango, bobinsana, ajo sacha and many forest trees. Some are added to ayahuasca; others are taken alone. According to Luna, what they share is that they cause visions, dizziness, vomiting or very vivid dreams [1]. A healer Luna worked with calls tobacco “the king of all plants”; most healers in the region work with tobacco alone. In his view the master’s task is not to teach medicine, which the plant does, but to protect the apprentice and show how the plants are prepared and taken [2].

The way to learn is a strict diet: no salt, sugar, fat, alcohol, pork, chicken, fruit or cold drinks; the staple is cooked plantain and smoked fish. The food is prepared by the apprentice himself or by a girl who has not yet begun to menstruate or a woman past menopause; the diet can last months or even years [1]. This is a cultural record; many of these plants are poisonous or potent.

Sources

  1. Luna, L. E. (1984). The concept of plants as teachers among four mestizo shamans of Iquitos, northeastern Peru. Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 11(2), 135–56. https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-8741(84)90036-9
