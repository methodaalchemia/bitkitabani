Concept of the day

Pachamama

Mother Earth of the Andes, “fed” with offerings in August.

The concept’s plantCocaErythroxylum cocaBolivia left the UN convention in 2011 in protest at the ban on leaf chewing and rejoined in 2013 with a reservation protecting that right.Plant page →

Pachamama is the earth-mother figure of the Andean peoples. Reverence for her is especially widespread on the Altiplano of Peru and Bolivia and is a tradition distinct from the Inca sun religion [1].

Pachamama is believed to need to “eat and drink”, so an offering called a “despacho” or “payment” is prepared for her. As described by a ritual master’s family from Peru, it includes Coca leaves, corn, seeds, sweets, white, pink and red carnation petals, a seashell and incense, while chicha or another drink is sprinkled on the earth. The family makes the offering in August [2].

Its meaning is reciprocity: the family shares its food and drink with the earth, and the earth feeds and protects them [2].

Sources

  1. Encyclopaedia Britannica: Pachamama
  2. Rufino Turpo, “An Offering to the Pachamama”, Smithsonian Folklife Festival, 2015
