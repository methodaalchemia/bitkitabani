Susto and mal aire

In Andean and Amazonian folk medicine: the soul fleeing the body after a fright, the blow of bad air, and illness caused by sorcery.

The concept’s plantMapachoNicotiana rusticaIn the Amazon tobacco is not a vice but a sacred teacher plant. In Anatolia the same plant became an everyday pleasure.Plant page →

The complaints most often brought to folk healers in northern Peru are illnesses such as “mal aire” or “mal viento” (bad air, bad wind), “susto” or “espanto” (fright), “mal ojo” (evil eye) and “daño” (harm caused by sorcery). Their causes are described as sudden changes in temperature, a great fright, a spell cast by someone else, or poisoned food [1]. Of the 510 medicinal plant species recorded in the region, 207, or 40 per cent, are used for such “magical” illnesses [2].

In southern Peru susto is described as the soul (ánimo) leaving the body after a great fright; children are thought to be most affected. It is treated with a night-time soul-calling ceremony: a mesa is set up, candles and incense are lit and the patient’s belongings are laid out [3].

In the Amazon, healers describe “mal aire” as the blow of a bad spirit, causing pallor, shivering, vomiting, diarrhoea and fever. “Cutipa” is harm from touching a powerful being such as a sloth or a river dolphin, or from breaking food taboos. Healers tell these apart by feeling the pulse and the hands and feet, and treat them with Mapacho tobacco, icaros, emetic plants, baths and diet [4].

Treatment ranges from a simple cleansing (limpia) with sprayed perfume and holy water in mild cases to an all-night mesa ceremony with the San Pedro cactus cactus in serious ones. Afterwards the patient is given a “flowering” bath and may receive a protective flask (seguro) of herbs, perfume, saints’ images and the patient’s own hair and nails [2]. Similar beliefs are known in Anatolia as fright, the evil eye and being “struck by a jinn”, for which people turn to ocak healers and lead pouring.
