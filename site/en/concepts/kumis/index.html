Kumis and the white offering

Fermented mare’s milk: a “white” offering to the sky, the four directions and the ancestors, and the spring kumis festivals.

The concept’s plantSilver birchBetula pendulaIn Central Asian Turkic “kayın” means birch; in Anatolia the name passed to a different tree, the beech (Fagus). Birch bark owes its whiteness to a compound called betulin.Plant page →

Kumis is an old Turkic drink made by fermenting mare’s milk. Among the Kazakhs the first kumis of the spring–summer season is called “qymyz muryndyq” and welcomed with a feast; kumis is sprinkled as a bloodless, “white” offering to the sky, the four directions and the mountains. The Buryats, Bashkirs and the Kazakhs of Mongolia have other names for the same festival [1].

In Fergana, Kipchaks, Kyrgyz and Kazakhs held a week-long kumis festival at the end of May: there was a contest for the best kumis, kumis was given as a gift in leather vessels, and kumis songs were sung [2]. The Bashkir kumis festival is held in June with the first milking of the mares; before going home people gather fresh herbs for themselves and their animals. Bashkir kumis ladles are carved from Silver birch wood, with bird figures on the handles [3].

At Ysyakh, the Sakha summer festival, young birches are planted along the host’s fences and kumis is served among them; kumis cups are raised nine times to the sky god and kumis is tossed three times toward the sky [4]. The old Turks offered the first bite and the first mare’s milk to the ancestors [5].
