Alastama (purifying with smoke)

The Kyrgyz practice of driving off evil spirits and purifying with juniper smoke; juniper incense is sacred in the Altai too.

The concept’s plantJuniperJuniperus excelsaGin takes its name and flavour from juniper berries (Dutch jenever).Plant page →

In Kyrgyz folk medicine the purification called alastap or ısırktap is done by burning Juniper leaves, and its smoke is used against evil spirits. One healer describes how, as the smoke rises, her voice “comes”, and then a knife is touched three times to the patient’s joints. Juniper smoke is also used as incense in homes, workplaces and bazaars [1].

In the same Kyrgyz material Syrian rue is burned as a calming “smoke treatment” [1]. The kinship with the Syrian rue incense burned against the evil eye in Anatolia is striking.

Juniper is sacred among other Turkic peoples too. The Bashkirs revered birch and juniper; a Russian traveller was scolded for breaking a juniper branch on Tura-Tau mountain without making a vow [2]. The commandments of Burkhanism (Ak Cañ), born in the Altai in 1904, say “burn juniper incense in censers” and “offer one another a juniper branch instead of tobacco”; the same movement decorated birches with five-coloured ribbons [3].

Like smudging in North America and limpia in the Andes, purification by smoke appears in similar forms in cultures far apart. Breathing smoke indoors for long is harmful to the lungs; burn incense briefly and in a ventilated place.
