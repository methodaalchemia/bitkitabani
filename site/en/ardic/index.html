In short

What is Juniper?

Juniper (Juniperus excelsa) is a plant of the Cupressaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Asia and Siberia. Part used: twigs, berries, tar.

What is Juniper used for?

Traditional use: Yörük nomads burn juniper twigs to smudge the home, the fold and the sick. Scientific evidence: traditional. The EMA recognises juniper berry as a mild diuretic and digestive aid as traditional uses.

Is Juniper safe?

Safety label: caution. Long-term or high-dose use of the berries can irritate the kidneys. Avoid in pregnancy and kidney disease. Juniper tar must not be swallowed. The EMA monograph does not recommend use under 18.

Traditional use

Anatolia

Yörük nomads burn juniper twigs to smudge the home, the fold and the sick. Cade oil from prickly juniper is applied to scabies, eczema and animal wounds.

Central Asia

In Kyrgyz, Kazakh and Mongol shamanism, arça smoke drives away evil spirits; places and people are cleansed with it before ceremonies.

In nature

Where it grows
Dry mountains of the Taurus and Central and Southern Anatolia; Greek juniper climbs to about 2,000 m.
Harvest season
The cone berries are gathered in autumn and winter, the twigs all year round.
Local names
ardıç, boylu ardıç, katran ardıcı, andız, arça

Mix-up risk

Some junipers (such as Juniperus sabina) are poisonous. Never use internally without knowing the exact species.

According to Baytop, herbalists mostly sell the cones of prickly juniper (Juniperus oxycedrus) in place of common juniper berries.

Preparation

The twigs are burned as incense; the cones are steeped; the tar is applied externally. The EMA monograph is valid only for Juniperus communis; J. excelsa and J. oxycedrus in our record are outside its scope. For tea, 2–2.5 g of cones in 150 ml of boiling water: as a urinary-tract flush 1–3 times a day (2.5–6 g a day), for indigestion 1–4 times a day (2.5–10 g a day). It is not recommended under 18; if the complaint lasts longer than two weeks, see a doctor. Juniper tar is not the subject of the monograph and is not swallowed.

What science says

The EMA recognises juniper berry as a mild diuretic and digestive aid as traditional uses. Antimicrobial effects have been seen in the lab.

Safety

Caution

Long-term or high-dose use of the berries can irritate the kidneys. Avoid in pregnancy and kidney disease. Juniper tar must not be swallowed. The EMA monograph does not recommend use under 18.
