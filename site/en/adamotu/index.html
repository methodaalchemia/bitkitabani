In short

What is Mandrake?

Mandrake (Mandragora officinarum) is a plant of the Solanaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: root.

What is Mandrake used for?

Traditional use: Its human-shaped root made it a plant of magic and love. Scientific evidence: traditional. The root contains scopolamine and hyoscyamine, whose sedative effect explains its ancient use in anaesthesia.

Is Mandrake safe?

Safety label: high risk. The whole plant is poisonous. The fruit and root can cause hallucinations, heart rhythm problems and respiratory arrest. Roots sold in markets as “aphrodisiacs” are dangerous.

Traditional use

Anatolia

Its human-shaped root made it a plant of magic and love. People believed it screamed when pulled up and had to be tied to a dog to be uprooted.

Ancient Mediterranean

In ancient Greece it was added to wine as an anaesthetic before surgery. The “dudaim” of the Hebrew Bible is thought to be mandrake.

In nature

Where it grows
Olive groves and field edges in the Aegean, Mediterranean and Southeast.
Harvest season
Not to be collected. Mandragora autumnalis flowers in autumn and winter; the fruit ripens in spring.
Local names
adamotu, abdüsselam otu, kankurutan

Mix-up risk

Its leaf rosette can be mistaken for edible greens; poisonings have happened this way.

Preparation

Not recommended to prepare or use.

What science says

The root contains scopolamine and hyoscyamine, whose sedative effect explains its ancient use in anaesthesia.
