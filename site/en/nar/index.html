In short

What is Pomegranate?

Pomegranate (Punica granatum) is a plant of the Lythraceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: fruit, rind, flowers.

What is Pomegranate used for?

Traditional use: A symbol of plenty; a pomegranate is smashed when moving into a new home or at New Year. Scientific evidence: limited. Rich in polyphenols.

Is Pomegranate safe?

Safety label: generally safe. The fruit is safe. Rind and root bark are toxic in high doses. Pomegranate juice may affect how some drugs are broken down.

Traditional use

Anatolia

A symbol of plenty; a pomegranate is smashed when moving into a new home or at New Year. The rind is boiled for diarrhoea; pomegranate molasses is used in cooking.

Ancient Mediterranean

The pomegranate seeds Persephone ate bound her to the underworld; the cycle of the seasons is told through this myth.

In nature

Where it grows
Aegean, Mediterranean and Southeast; the best known are Hicaz and Siirt pomegranates.
Harvest season
Fruit ripens September–November.
Local names
nar, enar, hicaz narı

Mix-up risk

Ornamental pomegranates bear small, sour fruit; there is no poisonous look-alike.

Preparation

Eaten fresh or juiced; the rind is dried and boiled; pomegranate molasses is made by reducing the juice.

What science says

Rich in polyphenols. Small studies report that pomegranate juice lowers blood pressure slightly; evidence for claims such as prostate cancer is insufficient.

Safety

Generally safe

The fruit is safe. Rind and root bark are toxic in high doses. Pomegranate juice may affect how some drugs are broken down.
