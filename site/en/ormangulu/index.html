In short

What is Pontic rhododendron?

Pontic rhododendron (Rhododendron ponticum) is a plant of the Ericaceae (fundagiller) family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: flower and nectar (mad honey); the whole plant is toxic.

What is Pontic rhododendron used for?

Traditional use: Mad honey made by bees from rhododendron flowers is eaten in small amounts as a folk remedy for stomach complaints, high blood pressure and sexual vigour. Scientific evidence: traditional. Grayanotoxins in the flowers, leaves and nectar keep sodium channels in cells open, which slows the heart and lowers blood pressure.

Is Pontic rhododendron safe?

Safety label: high risk. All parts of the plant and mad honey are toxic. Even a few spoonfuls of honey can cause dizziness, nausea, vomiting, sweating, a slow pulse, low blood pressure and fainting, usually soon after eating. It is treated in hospital and usually resolves within a day. In Turkey call 114 (poison centre) or 112 if poisoning is suspected.

Traditional use

Eastern Black Sea

Mad honey made by bees from rhododendron flowers is eaten in small amounts as a folk remedy for stomach complaints, high blood pressure and sexual vigour. This use is the main cause of mad honey poisoning.

History

Xenophon of Athens describes in the Anabasis how many Greek soldiers passing through the region around 400 BCE were poisoned by this honey (Baytop).

In nature

Where it grows
Damp forests of the eastern Black Sea region, in the understorey and clearings; purple-flowered R. ponticum and yellow-flowered R. luteum are common.
Harvest season
Flowers in late spring and early summer.
Local names
Komar, kara ağu, sarı ağu, zifin

Mix-up risk

Mad honey looks like ordinary blossom honey; know the source of honey bought in the eastern Black Sea region.

Preparation

Home use is not recommended; no part of the plant and no mad honey should be used as a remedy.

What science says

Grayanotoxins in the flowers, leaves and nectar keep sodium channels in cells open, which slows the heart and lowers blood pressure. According to Baytop, fresh honey is the main cause of poisoning; aged or boiled honey has not been reported to poison.

Safety

High risk

All parts of the plant and mad honey are toxic. Even a few spoonfuls of honey can cause dizziness, nausea, vomiting, sweating, a slow pulse, low blood pressure and fainting, usually soon after eating. It is treated in hospital and usually resolves within a day. In Turkey call 114 (poison centre) or 112 if poisoning is suspected.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Blood pressure and heart medicines (beta blockers, calcium channel blockers, digoxin)The slow pulse and low blood pressure caused by mad honey can become more severe.
