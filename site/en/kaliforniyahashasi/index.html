The Turkish name makes the plant sound more dangerous than it is: "Kaliforniya haşhaşı" brings the opium poppy to mind. The two are in the same family, but this plant contains no morphine or codeine; its alkaloids belong to a quite different group. The confusion the name creates works in both directions: it makes the plant needlessly frightening and, at the same time, seem more potent than it is. The indications the monograph gives stay modest: mild tension and help with sleep.

In short

What is California poppy?

California poppy (Eschscholzia californica Cham.) is a plant of the Papaveraceae (poppy family) family. Traditions that use it: North America. Part used: the aerial parts.

What is California poppy used for?

Traditional use: The EMA monograph places both indications under "traditional use": (1) relief of mild symptoms of mental stress; (2) to aid sleep. Scientific evidence: traditional. There is no randomised clinical trial of California poppy on its own.

Is California poppy safe?

Safety label: caution. Driving and operating machinery: the EU monograph states the plant may impair that ability and that affected patients should not drive or operate machinery; the Turkish monograph gives the same warning. For a plant used to aid sleep this is expected, but it should not be skipped over. Not for anyone hypersensitive to the active substance. Use should be stopped two weeks before surgery. Use under 18 has not been established; not recommended in pregnancy or lactation. If symptoms do not resolve over two weeks, consult a doctor or pharmacist. Persistent insomnia and tension deserve to have their cause investigated; they should not be managed with a herbal product for long.

Traditional use

Europe — two indications

The EMA monograph places both indications under "traditional use": (1) relief of mild symptoms of mental stress; (2) to aid sleep.

Turkey — official list

The Turkish monograph gives the part used as "the aerial parts" and adds two practical warnings: adverse effects may appear in situations requiring attention, such as driving and operating machinery, and use should be stopped two weeks before surgery. Use under 18 has not been established.

North America

The plant is native to California and northern Mexico and is the state flower of California. Its bright orange flowers are also grown in gardens; it has no wild distribution in Turkey and arrives as a product.

In nature

Where it grows
Native to California and northern Mexico; grown as a garden ornamental. No wild distribution in Turkey.
Harvest season
Bright orange flowers from spring to late summer; they open in sunshine and close in the evening and in cloudy weather.
Local names
Kaliforniya haşhaşı — the Turkish monograph gives one Turkish name.

Mix-up risk

⚠️ The real confusion is in the name: it is in the same family as the opium poppy (Papaver somniferum) but a different genus, and it contains no morphine. Garden cultivars also come in other colours; the EU monograph covers the species itself and draws no distinction between cultivars.

Preparation

The monograph describes oral preparations of the aerial parts. For the sleep indication it is taken before bed. If symptoms do not resolve over two weeks, consult a doctor or pharmacist. Use should be stopped two weeks before surgery. Use under 18 has not been established.

What science says

There is no randomised clinical trial of California poppy on its own. The human studies use multi-ingredient products: a prospective pilot study published in 2019 in Complementary Therapies in Medicine tested a combination of melatonin, vitamin B6 and medicinal plants in mild-to-moderate insomnia — a pilot study is a small preliminary trial with no comparison group, and a study like that measures the mixture, not any single plant in it, while melatonin is itself a substance with a known effect on sleep. The rest of the literature is at laboratory level: quantification of the alkaloids, intestinal permeability, and the plant’s effect on drug-metabolising enzymes. The last of these is worth noting: a study published in 2016 in Planta Medica showed that the plant and its alkaloids can modulate CYP enzymes, P-glycoprotein and the PXR receptor. That is a laboratory finding and does not prove an interaction will occur in a patient, but it is a reminder of why the monographs’ "no interactions reported" should not be taken as reassurance. The EU monograph rests both indications on long use. The evidence level is Traditional.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Sedative medicines (central nervous system depressants)The Turkish monograph states it may interact with this group. Using two things that act in the same direction can deepen drowsiness and loss of attention; alcohol acts the same way.
Drug metabolism (a laboratory finding)Not a reported interaction, but worth noting: a laboratory study showed the plant and its alkaloids can modulate CYP enzymes and P-glycoprotein. Those enzymes process a large share of all medicines. A cell-level finding does not prove an interaction in a patient; even so, anyone on regular medication would do well to mention it to their doctor.
No interactions reported in the EMA monographThe European monograph lists nothing; the Turkish one reports one group.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying California poppy.
