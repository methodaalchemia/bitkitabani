In short

What is Buckthorn?

Buckthorn (Rhamnus petiolaris ve diğer türler) is a plant of the Rhamnaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: fruit, bark.

What is Buckthorn used for?

Traditional use: The unripe berries give the bright yellow of carpets and kilims; yellow over-dyed with indigo becomes green. Scientific evidence: traditional. Contains anthraquinones, which stimulate the bowel and act as a laxative.

Is Buckthorn safe?

Safety label: caution. A strong laxative; long use causes a lazy bowel and loss of salts. Not for use in pregnancy.

Traditional use

Anatolia

The unripe berries give the bright yellow of carpets and kilims; yellow over-dyed with indigo becomes green. The bark is a folk laxative.

Europe

Its relative Rhamnus cathartica and alder buckthorn (Frangula) bark were long used as laxatives.

In nature

Where it grows
Dry slopes of Central and western Anatolia; once grown for dye.
Harvest season
Berries are picked green, July–August.
Local names
cehri, cehri dikeni, boya dikeni, akdiken

Mix-up risk

Many Rhamnus species look alike and all are laxative.

Preparation

Berries are dried and used in a dye bath.

What science says

Contains anthraquinones, which stimulate the bowel and act as a laxative. The EMA recognises the related Frangula bark for short-term constipation.

Safety

Caution

A strong laxative; long use causes a lazy bowel and loss of salts. Not for use in pregnancy.

Drug interactions
