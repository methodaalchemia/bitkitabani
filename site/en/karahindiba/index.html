In short

What is Dandelion?

Dandelion (Taraxacum officinale) is a plant of the Asteraceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Andes. Part used: leaves, root.

What is Dandelion used for?

Traditional use: In spring the leaves are eaten in salads and sautés. Scientific evidence: traditional. The EMA recognises the leaf as a mild diuretic and the root for mild digestive complaints as traditional uses.

Is Dandelion safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Generally safe. Avoid with bile duct obstruction or daisy-family allergy; it may strengthen diuretic medicines.

Traditional use

Anatolia

In spring the leaves are eaten in salads and sautés. In folk medicine it is known as a diuretic and liver cleanser.

Latin America

Diente de león is a common herb brewed for the liver and digestion.

In nature

Where it grows
Meadows, gardens and roadsides almost everywhere.
Harvest season
Leaves in spring, roots in autumn.
Local names
karahindiba, kara hindiba, arslandişi

Mix-up risk

Easily confused with chicory (Cichorium) and other yellow-flowered herbs. Most are edible, but do not pick without knowing them well.

Preparation

Leaves eaten fresh; the root is dried and steeped or roasted and drunk like coffee.

What science says

The EMA recognises the leaf as a mild diuretic and the root for mild digestive complaints as traditional uses. Human studies are very few.

Common mistakes

  • Combining it with diuretic medicines.
  • Eating it if allergic to the daisy family.
  • Drinking root tea with bile duct obstruction.

Frequently asked

How is dandelion eaten?

The young leaves are eaten raw in salads or cooked; the roasted root makes a coffee-like drink.

What is dandelion tea good for?

Traditionally drunk for digestion and as a mild diuretic.

Can people with kidney disease drink it?

It contains potassium and is diuretic; people with kidney disease should ask a doctor.

Safety

Generally safe

Generally safe. Avoid with bile duct obstruction or daisy-family allergy; it may strengthen diuretic medicines.
