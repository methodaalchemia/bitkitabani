BT-205 · Fabaceae (legume family)

Spiny restharrow

Ononis spinosa L.
Evidence: TraditionalCaution

The plant is named for its root, and several languages describe the same scene: the Turkish "kayışkıran" says the harness straps break, the English "restharrow" that the harrow stops, "stayplough" that the plough catches and holds. All three say one thing — in a field being worked, this plant’s root stops the job. And that root is exactly the part used medicinally. What makes the plant a nuisance in the field is what makes it a drug in the pharmacy.

Other names
The Turkish monograph gives the name "kayışkıran" (strap breaker); Ononis campestris Koch & Ziz is a synonym. The English names are many: restharrow, spiny restharrow, cammock, stayplough, wild liquorice. Most of the Turkish and English names say the same thing: the roots are so tough that ploughs and harness catch and stop.

In short

What is Spiny restharrow?

Spiny restharrow (Ononis spinosa L.) is a plant of the Fabaceae (legume family) family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Europe. Part used: the root.

What is Spiny restharrow used for?

Traditional use: The EMA monograph gives one indication, under "traditional use": to increase the amount of urine and so flush the urinary tract, as an adjuvant in minor urinary complaints. Scientific evidence: traditional. There is no randomised clinical trial of restharrow on its own, and the situation here is especially instructive because every human study is multi-ingredient.

Is Spiny restharrow safe?

Safety label: caution. Not to be used in oedema due to cardiac or renal insufficiency — that sentence in the Turkish monograph states the shared contraindication of diuretic plants: the plant works by increasing urine output, which where fluid intake has to be restricted is an intervention in the wrong direction. The EU monograph puts the same thing as "conditions where a reduced fluid intake is recommended". To be used with plenty of fluid. Hypersensitivity to the active substance can develop. Not taken internally by children under 12. Not recommended in pregnancy or lactation for lack of data. If urinary complaints worsen, or if fever, painful urination, spasm or blood in the urine occur, see a doctor. If symptoms do not resolve within a week, consult a doctor.

Traditional use

Europe — flushing the urinary tract

The EMA monograph gives one indication, under "traditional use": to increase the amount of urine and so flush the urinary tract, as an adjuvant in minor urinary complaints. The monograph also states the condition: adequate fluid intake is required for the amount of urine to increase.

Turkey — official list

The Turkish monograph gives the part used as "the roots" and sets two limits: not to be used in oedema due to cardiac or renal insufficiency — the shared contraindication of diuretic plants — and not to be taken internally by children under 12. To be used with plenty of fluid.

Anatolia

Restharrow is a spiny shrub common along field margins, dry meadows and roadsides in Turkey, recognisable by its pink-purple pea flowers. The part used medicinally is the root underground — and that root is what gives the plant its name.

In nature

Where it grows
Field margins, dry meadows and roadsides of Europe, North Africa and western Asia. Common in Turkey.
Harvest season
Pink-purple pea flowers from June to September. The root is lifted in autumn.
Local names
Kayışkıran — the Turkish monograph gives one Turkish name.

Mix-up risk

Many Ononis species grow in Turkey and all are called kayışkıran; the EU monograph covers O. spinosa only. The distinguishing feature is the spines: in O. spinosa the branches harden into them. One English name is also "wild liquorice", though the plant has no connection to liquorice (Glycyrrhiza).

Preparation

The monograph describes preparations of the root: herbal tea, infusion, decoction or macerate. The decisive condition is water: adequate fluid intake is required for the amount of urine to increase. If symptoms do not resolve within a week, consult a doctor. Not taken internally under 12.

What science says

There is no randomised clinical trial of restharrow on its own, and the situation here is especially instructive because every human study is multi-ingredient. Two examples: in the randomised trial published in 2026 in Urologia, a five-plant mixture including restharrow was compared with tamsulosin and the significant difference in urinary symptoms favoured tamsulosin. In a study published in 2024 in Medicina, a product containing boldine, Phyllanthus niruri and restharrow was tested added to tamsulosin for expelling distal ureteral stones: in 159 patients the stone expulsion rate was 84.8% in the add-on group against 52.5% with tamsulosin alone, and the need for analgesia was significantly lower in the add-on group. That finding looks favourable, but it carries two heavy limits: the study is retrospective (not randomised), so the two groups may have differed from the start; and it measures a three-ingredient product, not restharrow. The rest of the literature is at laboratory level. The EU monograph rests its indication on long use. The evidence level is Traditional.

Safety

Caution

Not to be used in oedema due to cardiac or renal insufficiency — that sentence in the Turkish monograph states the shared contraindication of diuretic plants: the plant works by increasing urine output, which where fluid intake has to be restricted is an intervention in the wrong direction. The EU monograph puts the same thing as "conditions where a reduced fluid intake is recommended". To be used with plenty of fluid. Hypersensitivity to the active substance can develop. Not taken internally by children under 12. Not recommended in pregnancy or lactation for lack of data. If urinary complaints worsen, or if fever, painful urination, spasm or blood in the urine occur, see a doctor. If symptoms do not resolve within a week, consult a doctor.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
LithiumThe Turkish monograph reports one interaction, with lithium. The reason is mechanical: lithium is cleared by the kidney, and a diuretic plant changes the kidney’s handling of fluid and salt. Because lithium’s effect depends on a narrow range of blood levels, that change matters. The same warning appears for horsetail and pumpkin seed in this catalog.
Diuretic medicinesNo interaction is reported, but the mechanism points the same way: using the two together can deepen fluid and electrolyte loss.
No interactions reported in the EMA monographThe European monograph lists nothing; the Turkish one reports one medicine.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying Spiny restharrow.
