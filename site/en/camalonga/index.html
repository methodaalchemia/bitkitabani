In short

What is Camalonga (yellow oleander)?

Camalonga (yellow oleander) (Thevetia peruviana (Cascabela thevetia)) is a plant of the Apocynaceae (zakkumgiller) family. Traditions that use it: Andes. Part used: seed (in ritual); the whole plant is poisonous.

What is Camalonga (yellow oleander) used for?

Traditional use: In northern Peruvian healing, dried seeds are steeped in wine, preferably church wine, and used for “mal aire” (bad air), epilepsy, “nerves” and illness caused by sorcery (daño). Scientific evidence: traditional. All parts of the plant, especially the seed kernel and the milky sap, contain cardiac glycosides such as thevetin A, thevetin B and peruvoside, which act like digoxin and disturb heart rhythm [4].

Is Camalonga (yellow oleander) safe?

Safety label: high risk. Highly poisonous. It causes vomiting, a slow pulse, heart block and fatal rhythm disorders. According to the international poisons monograph, the kernel of one fruit can kill a child and about ten an adult [4]. Because it is grown as an ornamental, keep the fruit away from children. If poisoning is suspected, call a poison centre or emergency number at once.

Traditional use

Northern Peru (curanderismo)

In northern Peruvian healing, dried seeds are steeped in wine, preferably church wine, and used for “mal aire” (bad air), epilepsy, “nerves” and illness caused by sorcery (daño). Leaf and stem baths are given against bad air, rheumatism and bad-luck spells; the source explicitly says they must not be swallowed [1].

History

Maichil is one of three plants, with San Pedro and ishpingo, whose use goes back to the Moche culture (AD 100–800); its seeds are depicted on Moche ceramics as leg rattles [1][2].

Iquitos

Among mestizo healers in the Amazon, those who work with camalonga seeds are called “camalongueros” [3].

In nature

Where it grows
Native to tropical America; grown as an ornamental in warm regions worldwide, including Turkey’s Mediterranean coast.
Harvest season
A perennial shrub or small tree; in the tropics it flowers all year.
Local names
Camalonga, maichil, mailchin, cabalonga

Mix-up risk

With its yellow, funnel-shaped flowers and narrow leaves it resembles oleander; both are in the same family and both are highly poisonous.

Preparation

Home use is strongly discouraged. No part of the plant should be taken by mouth.

What science says

All parts of the plant, especially the seed kernel and the milky sap, contain cardiac glycosides such as thevetin A, thevetin B and peruvoside, which act like digoxin and disturb heart rhythm [4]. The plant has also been reported to be neurotoxic [5].

Safety

High risk

Highly poisonous. It causes vomiting, a slow pulse, heart block and fatal rhythm disorders. According to the international poisons monograph, the kernel of one fruit can kill a child and about ten an adult [4]. Because it is grown as an ornamental, keep the fruit away from children. If poisoning is suspected, call a poison centre or emergency number at once.
