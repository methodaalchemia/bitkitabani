In short

What is Syrian rue?

Syrian rue (Peganum harmala) is a plant of the Nitrariaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Asia and Siberia. Part used: seeds, dried capsules.

What is Syrian rue used for?

Traditional use: Dried capsules are strung on a cord and hung by the door. Scientific evidence: traditional. The seeds contain β-carboline alkaloids such as harmine and harmaline, which reversibly inhibit the enzyme MAO-A.

Is Syrian rue safe?

Safety label: high risk. Drinking it can cause poisoning: vomiting, tremor, hallucinations and heart rhythm problems. It carries a risk of miscarriage. Because it is an MAO inhibitor, it interacts dangerously with antidepressants and certain foods.

Traditional use

Anatolia

Dried capsules are strung on a cord and hung by the door. The seeds are burned on embers as incense against the evil eye, especially at weddings, births and when moving into a new home.

Central Asia

In Kazakh and Uzbek tradition, adıraspan smoke cleanses the home and the newborn; the practice is linked to old shamanic rites.

In nature

Where it grows
Dry steppes, roadsides and overgrazed land in Central, Eastern and Southeastern Anatolia. Its native range stretches from Central Asia to North Africa.
Harvest season
Capsules are collected from late summer to autumn (August–October) once dry.
Local names
üzerlik, üzerlik otu, yüzerlik, nazar otu, hermel

Mix-up risk

Its look is distinctive, so it is rarely confused with other plants. The real risk is drinking the seeds without knowing the dose.

Preparation

Burned as incense. People sometimes drink small amounts at home; this is dangerous.

What science says

The seeds contain β-carboline alkaloids such as harmine and harmaline, which reversibly inhibit the enzyme MAO-A. They belong to the same family as the active compounds in the ayahuasca vine. There are no human studies of therapeutic use.

Safety

High risk

Drinking it can cause poisoning: vomiting, tremor, hallucinations and heart rhythm problems. It carries a risk of miscarriage. Because it is an MAO inhibitor, it interacts dangerously with antidepressants and certain foods.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAO inhibitors, tricyclics)Harmala alkaloids inhibit the MAO enzyme; combining them can cause serotonin syndrome.
Tyramine-rich foods (aged cheese, salami, fermented foods)With MAO blocked, tyramine cannot be broken down; blood pressure can rise suddenly and dangerously.
Stimulants and cold medicines (such as pseudoephedrine)Together with MAO inhibition, blood pressure and heart rate can rise dangerously.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying Syrian rue.
