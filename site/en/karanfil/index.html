In short

What is Clove?

Clove (Syzygium aromaticum) is a plant of the Myrtaceae (myrtle family) family. Traditions that use it: Asia and Siberia. Part used: unopened flower buds.

What is Clove used for?

Traditional use: On Indonesia’s Spice Islands a clove tree was planted when a child was born; the tree’s health was tied to the child’s. Scientific evidence: traditional. Eugenol in clove oil has long been used in dentistry as a local painkiller and antiseptic.

Is Clove safe?

Safety label: caution. Safe as a spice. Swallowing the pure oil can cause liver damage and seizures, especially in children. Applied often to the gums it irritates the tissue. May interact with blood thinners. The TİTCK monograph writes that use in large amounts may be genotoxic and carcinogenic and may lead to depression of the central nervous system, urinary disorders, acidosis, impaired liver function, coma, seizures and low blood sugar. It is not recommended in pregnancy or while breastfeeding.

Traditional use

Maluku Islands

On Indonesia’s Spice Islands a clove tree was planted when a child was born; the tree’s health was tied to the child’s.

Anatolia

For toothache a single clove is placed by the aching tooth; cloves go into winter teas, compotes and fruit drinks. Pushed into a lemon or orange, they are hung in rooms against bad smells.

In nature

Where it grows
Indonesia, Zanzibar and Madagascar; does not grow in Türkiye.
Harvest season
Buds are hand-picked when they turn pink, before opening.
Local names
karanfil, karanfil tanesi, bahar karanfili

Mix-up risk

The garden carnation (Dianthus) is an entirely different plant; the spice is the dried bud of a tree.

Preparation

The whole buds are boiled or ground. ⚠️ The EMA monograph covers not the bud but the essential oil (Caryophylli floris aetheroleum): for minor inflammation of the mouth and throat, a mouthwash containing 1–5% essential oil, several times a day; for the temporary pain of a decayed tooth, undiluted oil or a solution of at least 50% or a gel of 20%, soaked into a small piece of cotton wool and applied to the decayed part, repeated after 20 minutes and then every two hours if needed. Neither use is continued for longer than a week, and it is not recommended under 18. For the bud itself (Caryophylli flos) the EMA published a public statement rather than a monograph; there is no defined dose for the bud used as a spice.

What science says

Eugenol in clove oil has long been used in dentistry as a local painkiller and antiseptic. The EMA recognises clove oil for mouth and throat inflammation and toothache as traditional uses. Human studies of other claims are few.

Safety

Caution

Safe as a spice. Swallowing the pure oil can cause liver damage and seizures, especially in children. Applied often to the gums it irritates the tissue. May interact with blood thinners. The TİTCK monograph writes that use in large amounts may be genotoxic and carcinogenic and may lead to depression of the central nervous system, urinary disorders, acidosis, impaired liver function, coma, seizures and low blood sugar. It is not recommended in pregnancy or while breastfeeding.
