What is Cat’s claw?

Cat’s claw (Uncaria tomentosa) is a plant of the Rubiaceae family. Traditions that use it: Amazon. Part used: bark, root.

What is Cat’s claw used for?

Traditional use: One of the key healing plants of the Asháninka. Scientific evidence: limited. Contains oxindole alkaloids.

Is Cat’s claw safe?

Safety label: caution. Can interact with immunosuppressants, blood thinners and blood pressure medicines. Not for use in pregnancy.

Traditional use

Amazon

One of the key healing plants of the Asháninka. The bark is boiled and drunk for joint pain, inflammation, wounds and immunity.

In nature

Where it grows
Humid forests of the Peruvian Amazon.
Harvest season
Bark is collected all year.
Local names
cat’s claw; local names uña de gato, garabato

Mix-up risk

Uncaria guianensis is sold under the same name, but the two species differ chemically.

Preparation

The bark is boiled for 20–30 minutes.

What science says

Contains oxindole alkaloids. A few small studies in knee osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis reported slight benefit; larger studies are needed.

Safety

Caution

Can interact with immunosuppressants, blood thinners and blood pressure medicines. Not for use in pregnancy.
