In short

What is Bay laurel?

Bay laurel (Laurus nobilis) is a plant of the Lauraceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: leaves, berry oil.

What is Bay laurel used for?

Traditional use: In Hatay, laurel berry oil is mixed with olive oil to make the famous laurel soap. Scientific evidence: traditional. The leaf essential oil (cineole) shows antimicrobial effects in the lab.

Is Bay laurel safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Safe in cooking. A whole dried leaf can lodge in the throat if swallowed. Berry oil can cause allergy on sensitive skin.

Traditional use

Anatolia

In Hatay, laurel berry oil is mixed with olive oil to make the famous laurel soap. Leaves go into food and wardrobes; the oil is rubbed on rheumatic pain.

Ancient Greece

The sacred tree of Apollo. The Delphic oracle is said to have chewed bay leaves, and winners of the Pythian Games received laurel crowns.

In nature

Where it grows
Maquis and stream banks on the Mediterranean, Aegean and Black Sea coasts; most famously Hatay.
Harvest season
Leaves all year, berries October–November.
Local names
defne, teflan, tehnel

Mix-up risk

Cherry laurel (Prunus laurocerasus) and oleander leaves can look like bay; both are poisonous. A crushed bay leaf smells clearly of spice.

Preparation

Leaves in food or tea; berry oil externally.

What science says

The leaf essential oil (cineole) shows antimicrobial effects in the lab. Human studies are very few.

Safety

Generally safe

Safe in cooking. A whole dried leaf can lodge in the throat if swallowed. Berry oil can cause allergy on sensitive skin.
