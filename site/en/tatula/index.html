In short

What is Jimsonweed?

Jimsonweed (Datura stramonium) is a plant of the Solanaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Andes, North America. Part used: leaves, seeds (not to be collected).

What is Jimsonweed used for?

Traditional use: The leaves were once smoked like cigarettes for asthma. Scientific evidence: traditional. Contains atropine, hyoscyamine and scopolamine; its effect is the same as toé and henbane.

Is Jimsonweed safe?

Safety label: high risk. The whole plant is deadly poisonous. It can cause hallucinations, high fever, racing heart and coma. Deaths have been reported among teenagers trying it as a “natural drug”.

Traditional use

Anatolia

The leaves were once smoked like cigarettes for asthma. Children are poisoned when they eat the seeds by accident.

Andes and North America

Used as chamico in Andean sorcery and divination, and in coming-of-age rites by some North American Indigenous peoples.

In nature

Where it grows
Field edges, dumps and village margins; common throughout Türkiye.
Harvest season
Not to be collected. Flowers all summer.
Local names
tatula, boru çiçeği, şeytan elması, deli bat, domuz pıtrağı

Mix-up risk

Confused with toé; jimsonweed flowers point up, toé’s hang down. Its spiny capsule recalls a chestnut; children may eat the seeds.

Preparation

Not recommended to prepare or use.

What science says

Contains atropine, hyoscyamine and scopolamine; its effect is the same as toé and henbane.

Safety

High risk

The whole plant is deadly poisonous. It can cause hallucinations, high fever, racing heart and coma. Deaths have been reported among teenagers trying it as a “natural drug”.
