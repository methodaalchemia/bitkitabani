In short

What is Barley?

Barley (Hordeum vulgare L.) is a plant of the Poaceae (grass family) family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: the grain; the stem in craft use.

What is Barley used for?

Traditional use: In layer III of Göbekli Tepe, Hordeum cf. Scientific evidence: clinical. Barley beta-glucan is a soluble fibre shown in meta-analyses of randomised controlled trials to lower LDL cholesterol, non-HDL cholesterol and apolipoprotein B.

Is Barley safe?

Safety label: caution. COELIAC DISEASE AND GLUTEN — critical: barley contains hordein and is forbidden in coeliac disease. A gluten-free diet requires the complete removal of wheat, rye and barley. Barley water, barley malt, malt extract, malt vinegar and beer all contain gluten. The 2023 guideline of the American College of Gastroenterology names the three together. Because beta-glucan lowers post-meal blood glucose, theoretical caution is needed for people on diabetes medicines; no study measuring this interaction directly was found. No record was found for pregnancy, breastfeeding or toxicity.

Traditional use

Göbekli Tepe — and on the same slope today

In layer III of Göbekli Tepe, Hordeum cf. spontaneum, that is, wild barley, has been identified: fragments corresponding to an estimated 16 whole grains. Reinder Neef measured the grains; their being flat (0.7–1.4 mm) and broadly furrowed points to wild barley. The same report also records today’s vegetation: the slopes of the Göbekli Tepe plateau are still covered with a grass steppe dominated by wild barley. So this plant has stood on the same slope for eleven and a half thousand years.

Mardin — a wart ritual

In the rural villages of Mardin, barley grains are buried under the soil after certain special prayers have been recited over them, in the belief that this will make the warts on the body disappear. Bird cherry and grapevine are also used within the same belief. The local name is “ceh”.

Şırnak — folk medicine

Boiling barley seed and drinking it is used for bowel inflammation and constipation; its local names are “ca” and “ce”.

Mardin — craft

Barley stalks are used to make baskets and drinking straws. They also appear in the making of a musical instrument called “temir” or “kendırge”: it is held between the teeth and blown to make a sound similar to a zurna.

In nature

Where it grows
Native to the Fertile Crescent; grown in every region of Turkey. More tolerant than wheat of drought, salt and a short growing season, so it is sown on the steppe and at altitudes where wheat will not grow.
Harvest season
Mostly sown in autumn and harvested in June; spring sowing is also practised. It ripens earlier than wheat.
Local names
Ca, ce, ceh

Mix-up risk

Barley is told from wheat and rye by the ear: its awns are very long and run the whole length of it. Wild barley (Hordeum vulgare subsp. spontaneum) grows naturally in Anatolia and its ear shatters on its own when ripe; archaeologists distinguish domesticated from wild by that trait and by grain shape. All three contain gluten, so for coeliac disease there is no difference between them.

Preparation

There is no European Medicines Agency monograph and no NCCIH page, but barley is the only one of these four plants with an approved regulatory health claim. Under the European Union’s list of permitted health claims, foods with at least 1 g of beta-glucan per portion may carry the claim that beta-glucans contribute to the maintenance of normal blood cholesterol levels, with the stated benefit at an intake of 3 g a day. Foods with at least 4 g of beta-glucan per 30 g of available carbohydrate may carry the claim that eating them as part of a meal reduces the rise in blood glucose. The claim that barley grain fibre contributes to an increase in faecal bulk applies only to foods qualifying as “high fibre”.

What science says

Barley beta-glucan is a soluble fibre shown in meta-analyses of randomised controlled trials to lower LDL cholesterol, non-HDL cholesterol and apolipoprotein B. The effect of barley-derived soluble fibre on serum lipids has also been measured in humans, and in mildly hypercholesterolaemic adults it increased faecal bile acid excretion and short-chain fatty acid production. Of these four plants, barley is the only one with a claim that has regulatory approval.

Safety

Caution

COELIAC DISEASE AND GLUTEN — critical: barley contains hordein and is forbidden in coeliac disease. A gluten-free diet requires the complete removal of wheat, rye and barley. Barley water, barley malt, malt extract, malt vinegar and beer all contain gluten. The 2023 guideline of the American College of Gastroenterology names the three together. Because beta-glucan lowers post-meal blood glucose, theoretical caution is needed for people on diabetes medicines; no study measuring this interaction directly was found. No record was found for pregnancy, breastfeeding or toxicity.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Diabetes medicines and insulinBarley beta-glucan reduces the post-meal rise in blood glucose — an approved health claim in the European Union. No interaction study measuring this directly was found, but anyone on glucose-lowering medicines or insulin should speak to their doctor before changing their diet substantially.
Coeliac disease and gluten sensitivityThis is not an interaction but a firm restriction: barley contains hordein and must not be eaten at all in coeliac disease. That includes barley malt, malt extract, malt vinegar and beer.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying Barley.
