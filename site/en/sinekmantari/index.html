In short

What is Fly agaric?

Fly agaric (Amanita muscaria) is a plant of the Amanitaceae (mantar) family. Traditions that use it: Asia and Siberia. Part used: cap.

What is Fly agaric used for?

Traditional use: Koryak, Chukchi and Evenk shamans used dried fly agaric to enter trance and journey to the spirit world. Scientific evidence: traditional. A mushroom, not a plant.

Is Fly agaric safe?

Safety label: high risk. Poisonous: causes nausea, vomiting, hallucinations, seizures and loss of consciousness. The risk of confusing it with deadly Amanita species is very dangerous.

Traditional use

Siberia

Koryak, Chukchi and Evenk shamans used dried fly agaric to enter trance and journey to the spirit world.

Europe

Its name comes from soaking it in milk to kill flies.

In nature

Where it grows
Temperate forests of the Northern Hemisphere; in Türkiye, Black Sea and Marmara forests.
Harvest season
Appears August–November in pine and birch forests.
Local names
sinek mantarı, kırmızı benekli mantar

Mix-up risk

It belongs to the same genus as the deadly death cap (Amanita phalloides). Poisonings happen when it is mistaken for the edible Caesar’s mushroom (Amanita caesarea).

Preparation

Not recommended to prepare or use.

What science says

A mushroom, not a plant. It contains ibotenic acid and muscimol, which act on the brain’s GABA system. Drying converts ibotenic acid into the more potent muscimol.

Safety

High risk

Poisonous: causes nausea, vomiting, hallucinations, seizures and loss of consciousness. The risk of confusing it with deadly Amanita species is very dangerous.
