In short

What is White willow?

White willow (Salix alba) is a plant of the Salicaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, North America. Part used: bark.

What is White willow used for?

Traditional use: Willow bark is boiled for fever and pain in folk medicine; the thin branches are woven into baskets and cages. Scientific evidence: limited. Salicin in the bark is converted to salicylic acid in the body.

Is White willow safe?

Safety label: caution. Not for people allergic to aspirin or salicylates, people with asthma or stomach ulcers, or pregnant and breastfeeding women. Because of the risk of Reye’s syndrome, under-18s should not use it, especially with feverish viral illness. Bleeding risk may rise with blood thinners.

Traditional use

Anatolia

Willow bark is boiled for fever and pain in folk medicine; the thin branches are woven into baskets and cages. The weeping willow planted in cemeteries and by water is a symbol of mourning.

Ancient world and North America

Sumerian tablets and Egyptian papyri mention willow for pain and inflammation, and it appears in texts attributed to Hippocrates. Indigenous peoples of North America also used the bark of their own willows for pain and fever.

In nature

Where it grows
Stream and lake banks and damp ground throughout Türkiye.
Harvest season
Bark is stripped in spring, when the sap rises, from branches 2–3 years old.
Local names
söğüt, ak söğüt; weeping willow is a different species (Salix babylonica)

Mix-up risk

Weeping willow is not used medicinally. Willow can be confused with poplar; willow leaves are longer and narrower.

Preparation

Dried bark is soaked in cold water and boiled for 5–10 minutes; today it is mostly used as a standardised extract.

What science says

Salicin in the bark is converted to salicylic acid in the body. A systematic review (2009) found moderate evidence that willow bark extract helps low back pain, and inconsistent results for osteoarthritis. The EMA recognises the bark for short-term low back pain. It acts more slowly than aspirin, and its salicylate dose is far lower.

Safety

Caution

Not for people allergic to aspirin or salicylates, people with asthma or stomach ulcers, or pregnant and breastfeeding women. Because of the risk of Reye’s syndrome, under-18s should not use it, especially with feverish viral illness. Bleeding risk may rise with blood thinners.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Blood thinners (warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel)Salicylates may increase bleeding risk.
Painkillers (NSAIDs)Stomach side effects can add up.
MethotrexateSalicylates may slow its elimination.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying White willow.
