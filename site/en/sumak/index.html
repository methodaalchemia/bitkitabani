In short

What is Sumac?

Sumac (Rhus coriaria) is a plant of the Anacardiaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: fruit.

What is Sumac used for?

Traditional use: The red berries are ground into a sour spice for kebabs, salads and stuffed meatballs. Scientific evidence: limited. The fruit is rich in tannins and organic acids.

Is Sumac safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Safe as a spice. People allergic to cashew or pistachio may react.

Traditional use

Anatolia

The red berries are ground into a sour spice for kebabs, salads and stuffed meatballs. In folk use the fruit is boiled for diarrhoea and mouth sores.

Middle East

A core ingredient of za’atar; used instead of lemon for sourness.

In nature

Where it grows
Rocky slopes in Southeastern Anatolia, the Taurus and the Aegean.
Harvest season
Berry clusters are picked August–September when red.
Local names
sumak, somak, tetri

Mix-up risk

Some ornamental Rhus and Toxicodendron species irritate the skin. Spice sumac has red, hairy berries.

Preparation

Berries are dried and ground; also made into sherbet.

What science says

The fruit is rich in tannins and organic acids. Small clinical studies report slight drops in blood sugar and cholesterol; evidence is limited.

Safety

Generally safe

Safe as a spice. People allergic to cashew or pistachio may react.
