In short

What is Gotu kola?

Gotu kola (Centella asiatica) is a plant of the Apiaceae family. Traditions that use it: Asia and Siberia. Part used: leaves and aerial parts.

What is Gotu kola used for?

Traditional use: Ayurveda treats it as a herb that strengthens mind and memory; in Sri Lanka the leaves are eaten raw in a salad called gotu kola sambola. Scientific evidence: limited. The EMA recognises gotu kola for external traditional use to help minor wounds heal.

Is Gotu kola safe?

Safety label: caution. External use is generally safe and rarely causes contact allergy. Liver injury has been reported with long-term oral use. Not for people with liver disease, or pregnant or breastfeeding women. It can cause drowsiness.

Traditional use

India and Sri Lanka

Ayurveda treats it as a herb that strengthens mind and memory; in Sri Lanka the leaves are eaten raw in a salad called gotu kola sambola.

South-east Asia and cosmetics

In Thailand the leaf juice is drunk as a cooling drink. Korean cosmetics add it, as “cica”, to creams for wounds and redness.

In nature

Where it grows
Creeps over damp meadows, rice-field edges and ditches in tropical and subtropical Asia.
Harvest season
Leaves can be picked all year; most productive in the rainy season.
Local names
gotu kola; no settled Turkish name

Mix-up risk

In India the name brahmi is given both to gotu kola and to Bacopa monnieri; they are different plants. It has nothing to do with the kola nut.

Preparation

Fresh leaves are eaten as salad; the dried herb is made into tea. Creams use a standardised extract.

What science says

The EMA recognises gotu kola for external traditional use to help minor wounds heal. Small studies with its triterpene fraction report less swelling and heaviness in venous insufficiency. A meta-analysis (2017) on memory and cognition found no clear effect and a limited benefit for mood.

Safety

Caution

External use is generally safe and rarely causes contact allergy. Liver injury has been reported with long-term oral use. Not for people with liver disease, or pregnant or breastfeeding women. It can cause drowsiness.
