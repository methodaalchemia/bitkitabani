In short

What is Poison hemlock?

Poison hemlock (Conium maculatum) is a plant of the Apiaceae (carrot family) family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: whole plant; fruits and flower heads are the most toxic.

What is Poison hemlock used for?

Traditional use: Athens carried out death sentences with hemlock; Socrates died this way in 399 BCE. Scientific evidence: traditional. Piperidine alkaloids, chiefly coniine, block the signal from nerve to muscle.

Is Poison hemlock safe?

Safety label: high risk. The whole plant is toxic. It causes burning in the mouth, nausea, muscle weakness and paralysis rising from the legs; breathing can stop. Most poisonings happen when it is picked as parsley or wild carrot. In Turkey call 114 (poison centre) or 112 if poisoning is suspected.

Traditional use

Ancient Greece

Athens carried out death sentences with hemlock; Socrates died this way in 399 BCE.

Old medicine

The fruits were used as a painkiller; because the plant is so toxic, this use has been abandoned (Baytop).

In nature

Where it grows
Throughout Turkey in shady, damp places: waste ground, gardens, foot of walls, roadsides, ditches and stream banks (Baytop).
Harvest season
A biennial; white umbels open in summer.
Local names
Ağu, yılanotu

Mix-up risk

Confused with edible umbellifers such as parsley, wild carrot, chervil and fennel. The stem is hairless, hollow and spotted purple-brown; crushed leaves smell unpleasant, like cat urine (Baytop).

Preparation

Home use is not recommended; no part of the plant should be used.

What science says

Piperidine alkaloids, chiefly coniine, block the signal from nerve to muscle. Paralysis starts in the legs and moves upward; when it reaches the breathing muscles it can be fatal. According to Baytop, the herb contains a few parts per thousand of alkaloids and the fruit about one percent.

Safety

High risk

The whole plant is toxic. It causes burning in the mouth, nausea, muscle weakness and paralysis rising from the legs; breathing can stop. Most poisonings happen when it is picked as parsley or wild carrot. In Turkey call 114 (poison centre) or 112 if poisoning is suspected.
