Mayapple

Podophyllum peltatum
Evidence: ClinicalHigh risk

A single flower opens between its two umbrella-like leaves; it flowers in May and bears an apple-like fruit, hence “mayapple”.

Use

In short

What is Mayapple?

Mayapple (Podophyllum peltatum) is a plant of the Berberidaceae family. Traditions that use it: North America. Part used: root and rhizome (resin).

What is Mayapple used for?

Traditional use: Indigenous peoples used the root as a strong purgative, in small amounts against worms, and on warts. Scientific evidence: clinical. Podophyllotoxin stops cell division.

Is Mayapple safe?

Safety label: high risk. Root, leaves and unripe fruit are poisonous and can cause violent vomiting, diarrhoea, nerve damage and coma. Careless application of podophyllin resin to the skin has also caused poisoning. Never use in pregnancy; it can cause birth defects. Only the flesh of fully ripe fruit can be eaten, in small amounts; the seeds are poisonous.

Traditional use

North America

Indigenous peoples used the root as a strong purgative, in small amounts against worms, and on warts. The ripe yellow fruit was eaten in small amounts.

Modern medicine

Etoposide and teniposide, derived from podophyllotoxin in the root, are chemotherapy drugs used today for lung and testicular cancer and some leukaemias; podophyllotoxin cream is a prescription treatment for genital warts.

In nature

Where it grows
Shady floors of eastern North American deciduous forests, in wide colonies.
Harvest season
The flowers open in April–May; the fruit ripens in July–August, when it turns yellow.
Local names
It has no settled name in Turkish; the names mayıs elması and Amerikan adamotu are used

Mix-up risk

It is not related to true mandrake (Mandragora); the name “American mandrake” comes from the similar reputation of its root.

Preparation

Not used at home. Medicines are made from compounds purified from the root or made semi-synthetically.

What science says

Podophyllotoxin stops cell division. Its semi-synthetic derivative etoposide is on the World Health Organization’s list of essential medicines. Mayapple is a good example of a poisonous plant becoming a source of medicine; the plant itself is not used for treatment.

Safety

High risk

Root, leaves and unripe fruit are poisonous and can cause violent vomiting, diarrhoea, nerve damage and coma. Careless application of podophyllin resin to the skin has also caused poisoning.
