In short

What is White peony root?

White peony root (Paeonia lactiflora Pall.) is a plant of the Paeoniaceae (peony family) family. Traditions that use it: Asia and Siberia. Part used: the dried root.

What is White peony root used for?

Traditional use: "Uses supported by clinical data": none. Scientific evidence: limited. Peony root’s strongest evidence belongs to its extract licensed in China.

Is White peony root safe?

Safety label: caution. Contraindicated in pregnancy — the WHO monograph cites abortifacient activity on the basis of traditional reports. Not recommended in lactation or children for lack of data. The monograph has no adverse-reaction information; in the TGP meta-analysis the commonest effect reported is loose stool and diarrhoea. The monograph also relays from Chinese tradition that it is not to be combined with three plants (below). The wild peonies of Turkey are not covered by this monograph and their roots should not be dug from the wild.

Traditional use

WHO monograph (1999)

"Uses supported by clinical data": none. In pharmacopoeias and traditional systems: as an analgesic, anti-inflammatory and antispasmodic in amenorrhoea, dysmenorrhoea and chest and abdominal pain; headache, vertigo, calf cramps, liver disease and allergies. Maximum daily dose 6–15 g of root (standardised for paeoniflorin). Contraindicated in pregnancy: traditional reports point to abortifacient activity.

Europe — no monograph, a public statement

In 2015 the European Medicines Agency issued a public statement instead of a monograph for white peony root: with insufficient history of use and data in the EU, no monograph could be established. One of Chinese medicine’s most basic drugs has no official place in Europe.

China — a foundational drug

White peony root (báisháo) is among the oldest and most used drugs of Chinese medicine, an ingredient of blood- and liver-"nourishing" formulas. A standardised extract of the root ("total glucosides of paeony", TGP) is a licensed drug for rheumatoid arthritis in China — and the meta-analysis in the science section belongs to it.

In nature

Where it grows
Native to northern China, Mongolia and Siberia; widely cultivated in China. An ornamental in Turkey.
Harvest season
Large white-pink flowers in late spring; the root is lifted in autumn from 4–5-year-old plants.
Local names
Şakayık, beyaz şakayık; Chinese báisháo.

Mix-up risk

⚠️ The wild peonies of Turkey (Paeonia mascula, P. peregrina, P. tenuifolia) are separate species, some protected; the monograph is for P. lactiflora only. "Red peony root" (chìsháo) is the unprocessed root of the same species or P. veitchii, a separate drug in Chinese medicine.

Preparation

The WHO monograph gives a maximum daily dose of 6–15 g of dried root, standardised for paeoniflorin. The TGP capsules used in China are a prescription drug. Not in pregnancy; not recommended in lactation or in children either.

What science says

Peony root’s strongest evidence belongs to its extract licensed in China. A systematic review and meta-analysis published in 2021 in BMC Complementary Medicine and Therapies pooled 39 studies (3,680 patients) examining TGP added to standard drugs (methotrexate, leflunomide and others) in rheumatoid arthritis, and its main finding concerns safety: in the TGP-added groups hepatic adverse effects (RR 0.31; 95% CI 0.23–0.41) and leukopenia were significantly rarer. So the trials measure not whether peony treats the disease but whether it can reduce the drugs’ side effects; all were done in China and quality varies. The WHO monograph’s 1999 verdict of "no clinical data" is now dated for this extract, but still holds for the root as a tea. Evidence level Limited.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Fritillaria, Cuscuta and rhubarb (Rheum officinale)The WHO monograph, citing the Chinese pharmacopoeia, relays that peony root should not be combined with these three drugs — a traditional incompatibility rule, not pharmacological interaction data.
Anticoagulants (theoretical)The monograph lists "anticoagulant" among traditional uses; a theoretical note of overlap for anyone on a blood thinner.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying White peony root.
