In short

What is Common mallow?

Common mallow (Malva sylvestris) is a plant of the Malvaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Andes. Part used: leaves, flowers.

What is Common mallow used for?

Traditional use: In spring the leaves are sautéed or stuffed. Scientific evidence: traditional. The mucilage in its leaves coats the throat and stomach.

Is Common mallow safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Generally safe. The mucilage can delay absorption of other medicines; leave an hour between them.

Traditional use

Anatolia

In spring the leaves are sautéed or stuffed. Its tea is drunk for coughs, the throat and the stomach; a poultice goes on swellings.

Andes

In folk medicine built on hot–cold balance, malva is a “cooling” herb, given for fevers and bowel inflammation.

In nature

Where it grows
Village and garden edges, roadsides and waste ground throughout Türkiye.
Harvest season
Leaves March–May, flowers May–September.
Local names
ebegümeci, gömeç, kömeç, ebemkömeci

Mix-up risk

Dwarf mallow (Malva neglecta) is used the same way. It has no well-known poisonous look-alike.

Preparation

Dried leaves and flowers are steeped or soaked in cold water.

What science says

The mucilage in its leaves coats the throat and stomach. The EMA recognises mallow for mouth and throat irritation and dry cough as traditional uses.

Common mistakes

  • Picking from polluted roadsides: the plant can accumulate heavy metals.
  • Drinking it at the same time as medicines: mucilage can delay absorption; leave an hour between.

Frequently asked

How do I make mallow tea?

Steep dried leaves and flowers in boiling water, or soak them in cold water for a few hours to draw out more mucilage.

How is the mallow dish made?

Fresh leaves are cooked with onion and rice like spinach; a common spring dish in Anatolia.

Can I drink it in pregnancy?

There is not enough data; no risk is known when eaten as food.
