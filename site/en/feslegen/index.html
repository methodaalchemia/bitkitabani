In short

What is Basil?

Basil (Ocimum basilicum) is a plant of the Lamiaceae (mint family) family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Asia and Siberia. Part used: leaf, flowering tops.

What is Basil used for?

Traditional use: Grown in pots on windowsills and by doors; its scent is believed to keep flies away, and in folk songs it stands for the beloved. Scientific evidence: traditional. Antimicrobial, antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects have been shown in lab and animal studies.

Is Basil safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Safe in food and tea. The essential oil and concentrated supplements contain estragole and should not be used in pregnancy, while breastfeeding or by children. The leaves are rich in vitamin K; people on warfarin should not suddenly eat large amounts.

Traditional use

Anatolia

Grown in pots on windowsills and by doors; its scent is believed to keep flies away, and in folk songs it stands for the beloved. In eastern Anatolia purple basil is made into a sherbet and a tea.

Mediterranean and India

In Greek Orthodox churches holy water is sprinkled with a sprig of basil. In India, basil’s relative tulsi is a sacred plant.

In nature

Where it grows
It is an annual crop plant; it grows in gardens and pots all over Türkiye and does not tolerate frost.
Harvest season
Leaves are most aromatic June–September, just before flowering.
Local names
fesleğen, reyhan, mor reyhan

Mix-up risk

Purple basil is a dark variety of the same species. Tulsi (holy basil, Ocimum tenuiflorum) is a different species with hairy, clove-scented leaves.

Preparation

Fresh leaves go into food and salads; for tea, a pinch of fresh or dried leaves is steeped for 5 minutes.

What science says

Antimicrobial, antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects have been shown in lab and animal studies. Good clinical studies in people are almost non-existent. Estragole in basil oil causes liver cancer in animals at high doses, so in Europe long-term use of the oil and concentrated products is discouraged; the amounts in food are not considered a problem.

Safety

Generally safe

Safe in food and tea. The essential oil and concentrated supplements contain estragole and should not be used in pregnancy, while breastfeeding or by children. The leaves are rich in vitamin K; people on warfarin should not suddenly eat large amounts.
