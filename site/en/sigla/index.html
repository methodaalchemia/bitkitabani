In short

What is Oriental sweetgum?

Oriental sweetgum (Liquidambar orientalis) is a plant of the Altingiaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: resin from the trunk, bark.

What is Oriental sweetgum used for?

Traditional use: Around Köyceğiz in Muğla, resin is collected by scraping the bark. Scientific evidence: limited. The resin contains cinnamic acid and its derivatives.

Is Oriental sweetgum safe?

Safety label: caution. Can irritate sensitive skin and cause allergy. Internal use is not recommended.

Traditional use

Anatolia

Around Köyceğiz in Muğla, resin is collected by scraping the bark. Sığla oil is applied to wounds, eczema and scabies; the dried bark (günlük) is burned as incense.

Ancient Mediterranean

Storax resin appears among Egyptian embalming materials and was used in incense and perfume in ancient Greece.

In nature

Where it grows
Stream banks and plains with a high water table in Muğla (Köyceğiz, Marmaris, Fethiye), Aydın and Antalya. Outside Türkiye it grows naturally only on Rhodes.
Harvest season
Resin is collected in summer (June–October) by wounding the bark.
Local names
sığla, günlük ağacı, sığla yağı, buhur

Mix-up risk

Its leaves resemble plane-tree leaves and its fruit is a spiky ball. Sığla oil on sale is sometimes mixed with other resins.

Preparation

The resin is applied to the skin as an ointment or oil; the bark is burned as incense.

What science says

The resin contains cinnamic acid and its derivatives. Lab studies show antimicrobial and wound-healing effects; human studies are scarce.

Safety

Caution

Can irritate sensitive skin and cause allergy. Internal use is not recommended.

Conservation

IUCN
Endangered (EN)
Note
Its natural forests are small and fragmented, and shrinking through farmland clearance and drainage.
Alternative
Buy sığla oil from cooperatives or licensed producers.
