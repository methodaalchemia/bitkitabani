Huayruro

Ormosia spp.
Evidence: TraditionalHigh risk

The bright red-and-black seeds are still among the most common good-luck charms in Peru and are sold in markets as bracelets and necklaces [1].

Other names
Huayruro, huairuro; red-and-black amulet seed
Part used
Seed (as an amulet)
Region
Amazon
Use
Ritual and shamanic, Poisonous
In short

What is Huayruro?

Huayruro (Ormosia spp.) is a plant of the Fabaceae (baklagiller) family. Traditions that use it: Amazon. Part used: seed (as an amulet).

What is Huayruro used for?

Traditional use: A bracelet of dried red-and-black seeds is worn at all times on the left wrist against the evil eye (mal ojo) [1]. Scientific evidence: traditional. According to the source, Ormosia seeds contain potent poisonous compounds; today they are used mostly in jewellery and crafts [1].

Is Huayruro safe?

Safety label: high risk. The seeds are poisonous; do not chew or swallow them. Take care with necklaces and bracelets that small children could put in their mouths.

Traditional use

Northern Peru

A bracelet of dried red-and-black seeds is worn at all times on the left wrist against the evil eye (mal ojo) [1].

Amazon

The name huayruro is also given to Erythrina amazonica, whose seeds are made into a necklace that the shaman blesses and the wearer keeps for life, for protection from evil [1].

In nature

Where it grows
Lowland Amazon forests, from sea level to 500 m [1].
Harvest season
A tropical tree; the seeds are collected from ripe pods.
Local names
Huayruro, huairuro

Mix-up risk

It can be confused with other red-and-black seeds, such as the Erythrina species called by the same name and the highly poisonous rosary pea (Abrus precatorius).

Preparation

Used only as an amulet and ornament; the seeds must not be put in the mouth or eaten.

What science says

According to the source, Ormosia seeds contain potent poisonous compounds; today they are used mostly in jewellery and crafts [1].

Safety

High risk

The seeds are poisonous; do not chew or swallow them. Take care with necklaces and bracelets that small children could put in their mouths.
