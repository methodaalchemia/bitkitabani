In short

What is Elder?

Elder (Sambucus nigra) is a plant of the Viburnaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Andes. Part used: flowers, ripe berries.

What is Elder used for?

Traditional use: On the Black Sea coast the flowers become sherbet and the berries jam and syrup, drunk for colds and fever. Scientific evidence: limited. The EMA recognises elderflower for early cold symptoms as a traditional use.

Is Elder safe?

Safety label: caution. Raw berries, leaves, bark and stems contain compounds that release cyanide and cause nausea and vomiting. Always cook the berries.

Traditional use

Anatolia

On the Black Sea coast the flowers become sherbet and the berries jam and syrup, drunk for colds and fever.

Andes

The Andean native species Sambucus peruviana (sauco) is made into jams and drinks; its flowers are brewed for coughs.

In nature

Where it grows
Forest edges, hedges and stream banks in the Black Sea and Marmara regions.
Harvest season
Flowers May–June, berries August–September.
Local names
mürver, kara mürver, sambuka

Mix-up risk

Herbaceous dwarf elder (Sambucus ebulus) is poisonous. Black elder grows as a tree-like shrub.

Preparation

Flowers are steeped; berries must always be cooked into syrup or jam.

What science says

The EMA recognises elderflower for early cold symptoms as a traditional use. Small studies with berry extract reported shorter flu; the evidence is weak.

Safety

Caution

Raw berries, leaves, bark and stems contain compounds that release cyanide and cause nausea and vomiting. Always cook the berries.
