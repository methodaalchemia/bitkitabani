BT-310 · Malpighiaceae

Chaliponga

Diplopterys cabrerana (Cuatrec.) B.Gates
Evidence: TraditionalHigh risk

Taken by mouth on its own the plant has no effect: the DMT it carries is broken down in digestion. In ceremonial use the effect depends on the monoamine oxidase inhibitor in the other half of the mixture — the same substance that is the source of the dangerous drug interactions.

Other names
It has no Turkish name. Local names chaliponga, chagropanga, chagrupanga and oco yagé; in older botanical writing it also appears as Banisteriopsis rusbyana. In the north-west Amazon it enters the ayahuasca brew as an admixture plant. Neither the European Medicines Agency nor the Turkish agency has a monograph for this species. The DMT it contains is a controlled substance in Turkey.

In short

What is Chaliponga?

Chaliponga (Diplopterys cabrerana (Cuatrec.) B.Gates) is a plant of the Malpighiaceae family. Traditions that use it: Amazon. Part used: leaves.

What is Chaliponga used for?

Traditional use: An article in the Journal of Addictions Nursing describes ayahuasca as a mixture made from the bark of Banisteriopsis caapi, which carries a monoamine oxidase inhibitor, and the leaves of Psychotria viridis or Diplopterys cabrerana, which carry the serotonin 2A receptor agonist N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT). Scientific evidence: traditional. The sources relied on here contain no efficacy study of this species in humans; every publication found treats the plant as a component of the ayahuasca mixture.

Is Chaliponga safe?

Safety label: high risk. DMT is a controlled substance in Turkey, and this plant is not a treatment option. According to the sources relied on here the main dangers are psychotic episodes related to DMT and serotonin syndrome, which can be life-threatening. The monoamine oxidase inhibitor in the mixture leaves a person open to serotonergic toxicity when other serotonergic agents are taken as well. In a case report in Cureus a 46-year-old man took ayahuasca over three days and then over-the-counter dextromethorphan and diphenhydramine; he developed severe serotonin syndrome complicated by acute respiratory failure, needed emergency intubation and intensive care, and recovered with intravenous benzodiazepines and oral cyproheptadine. The sources also note that the content of such products is unregulated and unpredictable. There is no assessment for pregnancy, lactation or children, and none could apply.

Traditional use

North-west Amazon — admixture plant of the ayahuasca brew

An article in the Journal of Addictions Nursing describes ayahuasca as a mixture made from the bark of Banisteriopsis caapi, which carries a monoamine oxidase inhibitor, and the leaves of Psychotria viridis or Diplopterys cabrerana, which carry the serotonin 2A receptor agonist N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT). This plant is therefore used not alone but as one half of a pairing: one carries the active substance, the other the inhibitor that stops it being broken down in digestion.

The ceremonial context

The same article records ayahuasca as a traditional psychoactive sacrament used in shamanic ceremonies for centuries, and notes that its use has spread beyond the Amazon in recent years. This page describes the ceremonial use as a cultural record, not as a recommendation.

In nature

Where it grows
According to Kew’s records its range covers only five regions: northern Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru and Venezuela. It grows as a climber in the humid forests of the north-west Amazon.
Harvest season
It does not grow in Turkey; there is no collection calendar and no question of collecting it.
Local names
No Turkish name. Local names: chaliponga, chagropanga, oco yagé.

Mix-up risk

In the ayahuasca mixture it is used interchangeably with chacruna (Psychotria viridis) as the DMT source; the two plants are not related. Banisteriopsis caapi, the vine, is the other half of the mixture and carries different alkaloids.

Preparation

This page does not describe how it is prepared. The DMT the plant carries is a controlled substance in Turkey, so setting out how the brew is made would be setting out the preparation of an illegal substance. Nor does any official monograph set an accepted dose.

What science says

The sources relied on here contain no efficacy study of this species in humans; every publication found treats the plant as a component of the ayahuasca mixture. A review in Mental Health Clinician assesses the literature discussing ayahuasca in contemporary psychiatric illness. The article in the Journal of Addictions Nursing stresses that the consequences of the mixture remain uncertain because of poor quality control, unpredictability and polydrug interactions. A 2026 study in iScience used machine learning on herbarium leaves to recover folk classifications of Banisteriopsis caapi, with this species as an outgroup; it concerns botanical discrimination, not treatment.

High risk

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs), triptans, tramadol, dextromethorphan, linezolidTogether with the monoamine oxidase inhibitor in the brew these can cause serotonin syndrome; in one reported case the course led to respiratory failure.
Monoamine oxidase inhibitor medicinesThe effects add up, carrying a risk of hypertensive crisis and serotonergic toxicity.
Tyramine-rich foods (aged cheeses, fermented products)In the presence of a monoamine oxidase inhibitor these can raise blood pressure.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying Chaliponga.
