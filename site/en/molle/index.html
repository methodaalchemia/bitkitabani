Peruvian pepper tree

In short

What is Peruvian pepper tree?

Peruvian pepper tree (Schinus molle) is a plant of the Anacardiaceae family. Traditions that use it: Andes. Part used: fruit, leaves, resin.

What is Peruvian pepper tree used for?

Traditional use: The Incas made a fermented drink (chicha de molle) from the berries. Scientific evidence: traditional. The leaf oil shows antibacterial and insect-repellent effects in the lab.

Is Peruvian pepper tree safe?

Safety label: caution. Eating too many berries can upset the stomach. Can cause allergy in people allergic to cashew or mango.

Traditional use

Andes

The Incas made a fermented drink (chicha de molle) from the berries. The leaves are rubbed on the body in limpia rites and added to baths for rheumatism.

Andes

Archaeology documents large-scale molle beer production at Cerro Baúl in the Wari period.

In nature

Where it grows
Dry Andean valleys; now an ornamental in many places, including the Mediterranean.
Harvest season
Berries ripen in late winter.
Local names
No Turkish folk name. Local names: molle, mulli, pirul

Mix-up risk

The Brazilian pepper tree (Schinus terebinthifolia) is similar and also sold as pink pepper.

Preparation

Berries are soaked and fermented; leaves are boiled and added to bath water.

What science says

The leaf oil shows antibacterial and insect-repellent effects in the lab. There are no human studies.

Safety

Caution

Eating too many berries can upset the stomach. Can cause allergy in people allergic to cashew or mango.
