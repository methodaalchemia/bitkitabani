Chuchuhuasi

Maytenus macrocarpa
Evidence: TraditionalCaution

Its name is said to mean “trembling back” in Quechua; it is believed to heal a back shaking with pain.

In short

What is Chuchuhuasi?

Chuchuhuasi (Maytenus macrocarpa) is a plant of the Celastraceae family. Traditions that use it: Amazon. Part used: bark.

What is Chuchuhuasi used for?

Traditional use: The bark is steeped in sugarcane spirit (aguardiente) and drunk for rheumatism, back pain and fatigue. Scientific evidence: traditional. Lab studies show anti-inflammatory effects.

Is Chuchuhuasi safe?

Safety label: caution. It is prepared with alcohol, so the risks of alcohol apply. Not for use in pregnancy or liver disease.

Traditional use

Amazon

The bark is steeped in sugarcane spirit (aguardiente) and drunk for rheumatism, back pain and fatigue. It is the best-known herbal liquor sold in Iquitos.

In nature

Where it grows
Tall forest trees of the Peruvian Amazon.
Harvest season
Bark is collected all year.
Local names
No Turkish folk name. Local names: chuchuhuasi, chuchuhuasha

Mix-up risk

Bark on the market can come from several different Maytenus species.

Preparation

The bark is steeped in alcohol for a few days.

What science says

Lab studies show anti-inflammatory effects. There are no human studies.

Safety

Caution

It is prepared with alcohol, so the risks of alcohol apply. Not for use in pregnancy or liver disease.
