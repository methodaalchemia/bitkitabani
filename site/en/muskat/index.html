In short

What is Nutmeg?

Nutmeg (Myristica fragrans) is a plant of the Myristicaceae family. Traditions that use it: Asia and Siberia. Part used: seed and aril (mace).

What is Nutmeg used for?

Traditional use: Native to the Banda Islands. Scientific evidence: traditional. Myristicin and elemicin in nutmeg affect the nervous system at high doses.

Is Nutmeg safe?

Safety label: caution. Pinches used in cooking are safe. High doses cause poisoning: nausea, palpitations, dry mouth, anxiety and hallucinations start after a few hours and can last a day or two. Using it to get high is dangerous. Avoid large amounts in pregnancy. Keep out of reach of children.

Traditional use

Indonesia

Native to the Banda Islands. In 1621 the Dutch East India Company killed or deported most of the Banda people to seize the nutmeg monopoly.

Ottoman, Arab and European kitchens

It went into Ottoman electuaries and sherbets; in medieval Europe it was a costly spice believed to protect against plague. Today it flavours béchamel, meatballs and biscuits.

In nature

Where it grows
Native to Indonesia’s Maluku (Spice) Islands; now also grown in Grenada, India and Sri Lanka.
Harvest season
In the tropics trees fruit several times a year; when the ripe fruit splits, the seed with its red aril appears.
Local names
muskat, küçük Hindistan cevizi; mace is besbase

Mix-up risk

Coconut (Cocos nucifera) is a completely different plant. Mace is the red covering of the same seed and contains similar compounds.

Preparation

The whole seed is grated just before use; a pinch is enough for a dish.

What science says

Myristicin and elemicin in nutmeg affect the nervous system at high doses. Antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory effects have been shown in the lab, but there are almost no clinical studies in people. Poisoning reports show dizziness, palpitations, vomiting and hallucinations with amounts above about 5 grams (two teaspoons).

Safety

Caution

Pinches used in cooking are safe. High doses cause poisoning: nausea, palpitations, dry mouth, anxiety and hallucinations start after a few hours and can last a day or two. Using it to get high is dangerous. Avoid large amounts in pregnancy. Keep out of reach of children.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Medicines acting on the nervous system, and alcoholAt high doses, nutmeg’s effects may be stronger.
Antidepressants (especially MAO inhibitors)Myristicin is a weak MAO inhibitor; interaction is possible at high doses.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying Nutmeg.
