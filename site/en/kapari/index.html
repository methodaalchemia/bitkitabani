In short

What is Caper?

Caper (Capparis spinosa) is a plant of the Capparaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: buds, fruit, root.

What is Caper used for?

Traditional use: The buds are pickled and used in cooking. Scientific evidence: traditional. Contains flavonoids such as rutin and kaempferol.

Is Caper safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Safe as food.

Traditional use

Anatolia

The buds are pickled and used in cooking. In folk use the root is boiled for rheumatism and the spleen.

Central Asia

In Uyghur folk medicine caper fruit is used for joint pain.

In nature

Where it grows
Dry, rocky places, wall bases and roadsides in the Southeast, Mediterranean and Central Anatolia.
Harvest season
Buds are picked early in the morning before they open, May–August.
Local names
kapari, kebere, gebere, keper

Mix-up risk

A spiny shrub, easy to recognise by its distinctive white-pink flowers.

Preparation

Buds are salted and pickled.

What science says

Contains flavonoids such as rutin and kaempferol. Beyond lab studies, human research is very scarce.

Safety

Generally safe

Safe as food.
