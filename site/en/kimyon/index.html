In short

What is Cumin?

Cumin (Cuminum cyminum) is a plant of the Apiaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Andes. Part used: seeds (fruit).

What is Cumin used for?

Traditional use: The spice of köfte, sucuk and dried-bean dishes. Scientific evidence: limited. Contains cuminaldehyde.

Is Cumin safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Safe as a spice.

Traditional use

Anatolia

The spice of köfte, sucuk and dried-bean dishes. The seeds are brewed for gas and indigestion.

Andes

Arriving with the Spanish, comino became a basic spice of Peruvian cooking.

In nature

Where it grows
A crop plant, sown in Central and western Anatolia.
Harvest season
Seeds are harvested June–July.
Local names
kimyon, kemmun, acı kimyon

Mix-up risk

Can be confused with caraway (Carum carvi) and black seed; cumin seeds are longer and paler.

Preparation

Seeds are roasted and ground, or steeped.

What science says

Contains cuminaldehyde. There are small studies on irritable bowel and weight management; the evidence is weak.

Safety

Generally safe

Safe as a spice.
