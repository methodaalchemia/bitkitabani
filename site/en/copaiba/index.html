In short

What is Copaiba?

Copaiba (Copaifera officinalis) is a plant of the Fabaceae family. Traditions that use it: Amazon. Part used: trunk resin (oil).

What is Copaiba used for?

Traditional use: The oily resin flowing from a hole drilled in the trunk is applied to wounds, skin disease and sore throats; a few drops are swallowed. Scientific evidence: limited. Contains sesquiterpenes such as beta-caryophyllene.

Is Copaiba safe?

Safety label: caution. Drinking it can cause nausea and diarrhoea. Can cause skin allergy.

Traditional use

Amazon

The oily resin flowing from a hole drilled in the trunk is applied to wounds, skin disease and sore throats; a few drops are swallowed.

Europe

From the 17th century it was used in European pharmacies for urinary tract inflammation.

In nature

Where it grows
The Amazon basin and Brazilian forests.
Harvest season
Resin is collected all year, with rest periods for the tree.
Local names
No Turkish folk name. Local names: copaiba, kupayiwa

Mix-up risk

Copaiba oils on the market are sometimes cut with other oils.

Preparation

The resin is applied directly or a few drops are taken.

What science says

Contains sesquiterpenes such as beta-caryophyllene. Lab and animal studies show anti-inflammatory and wound-healing effects; human studies are few.

Safety

Caution

Drinking it can cause nausea and diarrhoea. Can cause skin allergy.
