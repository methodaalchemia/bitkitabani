In short

What is Saw palmetto?

Saw palmetto (Serenoa repens) is a plant of the Arecaceae family. Traditions that use it: North America. Part used: fruit.

What is Saw palmetto used for?

Traditional use: The Seminole of Florida ate the fruit as food. Scientific evidence: limited. Large, well-designed trials showed saw palmetto extract works no better than placebo for urinary symptoms from prostate enlargement.

Is Saw palmetto safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Generally safe; can cause stomach complaints.

Traditional use

North America

The Seminole of Florida ate the fruit as food.

Today

The fruit extract is widely used for urinary complaints from benign prostate enlargement.

In nature

Where it grows
The US southeast, especially Florida’s sandy pine forests.
Harvest season
The fruit is gathered between August and October.
Local names
cüce palmiye, testere palmiyesi

Mix-up risk

Its leaf stalks are toothed like a saw, which gives it its name.

Preparation

It is not prepared as a tea: the compounds held responsible for the effect dissolve in fat, not in water. The forms accepted in the EMA assessment are soft extracts — 160 mg twice a day (hexane extract), or 320 mg once a day (ethanol extract). For adults only; if there is fever, cramps or blood in the urine, a doctor is needed rather than a product, because these symptoms may be a sign of something other than prostate enlargement.

What science says

Large, well-designed trials showed saw palmetto extract works no better than placebo for urinary symptoms from prostate enlargement. The EMA recognises this use for some extracts.

Safety

Generally safe

Generally safe; can cause stomach complaints.

Conservation

Note
Wild harvesting in Florida requires a permit; unauthorised harvesting reduces a food source for wildlife.
