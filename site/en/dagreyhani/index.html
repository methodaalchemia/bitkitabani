Blue mint bush

Ziziphora clinopodioides Lam.
Evidence: LimitedCaution

According to the 2025 review the genus Ziziphora has 31 taxa in the world and 11 in Türkiye, and Ziziphora clinopodioides is the only species of the genus that is perennial; the others are annual herbs.

Other names
In Türkiye the genus Ziziphora is known by the names dağ reyhanı, nane ruhu, firüskül, kır nanesi and karınağrısı otu; these names are shared by several species of the genus. English blue mint bush, mountain mint. The Kew world plant checklist accepts several subspecies of the species (subsp. bungeana, subsp. rigida and others). In short

What is Blue mint bush?

Blue mint bush (Ziziphora clinopodioides Lam.) is a plant of the Lamiaceae (mint family) family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean, Asia and Siberia. Part used: flowering aerial parts (leaf, flower, stem); the essential oil.

What is Blue mint bush used for?

Traditional use: According to the 2025 review in the journal Records of Natural Products, in Türkiye the aerial parts of Ziziphora species are used as a vegetable, as herbal tea and as a flavouring in dishes; the infusion is drunk for colds, coughs and digestive complaints. Scientific evidence: limited. All of the studies reported for the species in the 2025 review are preclinical; no clinical trial in humans is reported.

Is Blue mint bush safe?

Safety label: caution. ⚠️ In many samples of the species the main constituent of the essential oil is pulegone (reported contents 31.86–70.4 per cent; in some samples, however, very low). According to the EMA’s public statement on pulegone and menthofuran (2016), the main target organ of these substances is the liver; drinking about 10 ml of pennyroyal oil, which is high in pulegone, has caused moderate to severe poisoning, and more than 15 ml has caused death. The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies pulegone in Group 2B (possibly carcinogenic to humans). The EMA recommends 0.75 mg/kg body weight per day as the acceptable limit for the sum of pulegone and menthofuran in herbal medicines; this comes to 37.5 mg a day in life-long use for a 50 kg adult and is scaled down with body weight in children. The essential oil should not be drunk; medicinal use of the plant should be avoided in pregnancy, while breastfeeding and in children, because there are no human safety data for the species. People with liver disease should keep away from strong teas and extracts. If the essential oil has been swallowed, call 114 (the National Poison Information Centre of Türkiye) or 112.

Traditional use

Türkiye — tea, wild vegetable and seasoning

According to the 2025 review in the journal Records of Natural Products, in Türkiye the aerial parts of Ziziphora species are used as a vegetable, as herbal tea and as a flavouring in dishes; the infusion is drunk for colds, coughs and digestive complaints. A 2026 study in Scientific Reports describes the species as a plant used as a traditional tea in Türkiye.

A wide geography — records of folk medicine

The same review gives the traditional uses recorded for the species collectively: as an infusion it is used as an appetiser, anti-emetic, sedative, expectorant, carminative and antipyretic; and in headache, colds, cough, insomnia, high blood pressure, cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, diarrhoea and wounds. These are records of folk use, not proven indications.

Iran — digestive complaints

A 2017 study on samples from four provinces of western Iran refers to the traditional use of the essential oil in gastrointestinal diseases. A 2018 animal study from Pakistan likewise writes that the plant is used in folk medicine as an anti-inflammatory.

In nature

Where it grows
According to the Kew world plant checklist it is a plant of temperate Asia: it ranges across Türkiye, the Caucasus, Iran, Iraq, Lebanon-Syria, the countries of Central Asia, Afghanistan, Pakistan, the western Himalaya, Xinjiang, Mongolia and as far as southern Siberia. In Türkiye the species itself and four subspecies are recorded (subsp. elbursensis, filicaulis, kurdica, rigida).
Harvest season
It is a perennial, mat-forming, densely hairy plant with its flowers gathered in heads. The sources examined do not give a specific collecting month; the studies were carried out on the flowering aerial parts.
Local names
Dağ reyhanı, nane ruhu, firüskül, kır nanesi, karınağrısı otu (names shared across the genus Ziziphora).

Mix-up risk

The same Turkish names are also used for the other species of the genus (Z. tenuior, Z. capitata, Z. persica, Z. taurica); the name alone does not identify the species. Dağ reyhanı differs from the other Ziziphora species, which are annual, in being perennial. Because the composition of the essential oil also varies from sample to sample (pulegone, carvacrol, thymol or 1,8-cineole type), the content of one sample cannot be carried over to another.

Preparation

The aerial parts are infused to make tea or added to food to give flavour. Because there is no monograph for this species on the EMA or TİTCK lists, there is no accepted dose. The composition of the essential oil varies greatly from sample to sample, and in many samples the main constituent is pulegone; for pulegone and menthofuran the EMA recommends that total daily intake should not exceed 0.75 mg per kilogram of body weight. This page gives no recipe for oral use of the essential oil.

What science says

All of the studies reported for the species in the 2025 review are preclinical; no clinical trial in humans is reported. The composition of the essential oil separates markedly by chemotype: in the studies in the review the pulegone content is between 31.86 and 70.4 per cent; in a sample from Türkiye examined in 2026, on the other hand, the main constituents were 1,8-cineole (28.2 per cent) and terpinen-4-ol (12.0 per cent), with pulegone remaining at 7.9 per cent. In three of the western Iranian samples the main constituent was carvacrol (65.11–74.29 per cent) and in one it was thymol (55.32–55.60 per cent); these oils inhibited six food-borne bacteria at a concentration of 0.03–0.04 per cent. In rats, extracts of the plant reduced experimental rheumatoid arthritis and paw oedema. A 2026 review summarises the vasodilatory, anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects of the flavonoids and the essential oil relevant to cardiovascular diseases through mechanisms at the cell and animal level. In the sample from Türkiye the α-amylase inhibition of the essential oil remained at 29.3 per cent, and all the enzyme effects were lower than those of the reference drugs. These findings have not been tested in humans.

Safety

Caution

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Drug interactionsNo drug interaction study has been carried out for this species. The EMA statement notes that pulegone targets the liver; for this reason people with liver disease and those taking medicines that can affect the liver should not use pulegone-containing oil and concentrated extracts without consulting a doctor.
Blood pressure medicines (theoretical)The plant extract has shown a relaxant effect on rat arteries and it is used among the people for high blood pressure; there are no interaction data in humans. It is appropriate for people taking blood pressure medication to be careful with concentrated products.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying Blue mint bush.
