In short

What is Henbane?

Henbane (Hyoscyamus niger) is a plant of the Solanaceae family. Traditions that use it: Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: seeds, leaves.

What is Henbane used for?

Traditional use: For toothache, seeds were burned on embers and the smoke drawn into the mouth to “drive out the tooth worm”. Scientific evidence: traditional. It contains tropane alkaloids such as hyoscyamine and scopolamine, today the source of drugs for nausea, cramps and eye exams; but dosing the plant itself is impossible.

Is Henbane safe?

Safety label: high risk. The whole plant is poisonous. It can cause dry mouth, fever, hallucinations, racing heart and death. Poisonings have followed accidental picking and eating.

Traditional use

Anatolia

For toothache, seeds were burned on embers and the smoke drawn into the mouth to “drive out the tooth worm”. People knew it was poisonous.

Europe

Named among the ingredients of medieval “witches’ ointments”. In Germany it was added to beer; it is thought to be one reason for the 1516 Beer Purity Law.

In nature

Where it grows
Village edges, by barns, roadsides and waste ground throughout Türkiye.
Harvest season
Not to be collected. Flowers May–August.
Local names
banotu, ban otu, kara banotu, deli banotu

Mix-up risk

Poisonings, including of children, have followed mistaking it for edible wild greens.

Preparation

Not recommended to prepare or use.

What science says

It contains tropane alkaloids such as hyoscyamine and scopolamine, today the source of drugs for nausea, cramps and eye exams; but dosing the plant itself is impossible.

Safety

High risk
