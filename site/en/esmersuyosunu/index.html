Bladderwrack

Fucus vesiculosus L.
Evidence: TraditionalCaution

This is one of the few records in the catalog where the content requirement is given as a number: under the European Pharmacopoeia the iodine content of Fucus vesiculosus powder must be between 0.03% and 0.2%. There is both a lower and an upper limit, because iodine is responsible for both the effect and the risk. It is a good illustration of why "natural means harmless" falls short: what creates the danger here is not an additive or a contaminant but the organism’s own composition — and safe use depends on that composition being measured.

In short

What is Bladderwrack?

Bladderwrack (Fucus vesiculosus L.) is a plant of the Fucaceae family. Traditions that use it: Europe. Part used: the thallus.

What is Bladderwrack used for?

Traditional use: The EMA monograph gives one indication, under "traditional use": as an adjuvant to a reduced-calorie diet to help weight loss in overweight adults, after serious conditions have been excluded by a medical doctor. Scientific evidence: traditional. The monograph’s indication is helping with weight loss, and there is a randomised trial that tests that indication directly.

Is Bladderwrack safe?

Safety label: caution. Almost every warning in this record comes from one thing: iodine. The Turkish monograph says it is not to be used in hyperthyroidism, Graves’ or Basedow’s disease, Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, after partial removal of the thyroid gland, in iodine excess, halogen sensitivity, cancer or tuberculosis; nor in cardiovascular and coronary disease, renal failure or post-menopausal osteoporosis. The monograph gives the reason in one sentence: exceeding the daily iodine limit induces hyperthyroidism or worsens thyroid disease. The high iodine content can also cause allergy in sensitive people. It must not be taken alongside other preparations containing Fucus, iodine-containing preparations, or thyroid medicines. Not for anyone hypersensitive to the active substance. Not recommended under 18; not to be used in pregnancy or lactation — iodine balance in pregnancy is particularly delicate. If symptoms worsen, consult a doctor or pharmacist. The TİTCK monograph writes that it should be stopped two weeks before surgery; it interacts with thyroid medicines, blood thinners, antiplatelet medicines, diabetes medicines, lithium and medicines metabolised in the liver by the CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 enzymes.

Traditional use

Europe — an adjuvant to dieting

The EMA monograph gives one indication, under "traditional use": as an adjuvant to a reduced-calorie diet to help weight loss in overweight adults, after serious conditions have been excluded by a medical doctor. The wording is careful: the plant is not a weight-loss method in itself but an adjuvant to the diet.

Turkey — official list and iodine

The Turkish monograph gives the part used as "the thallus" and carries one of the longest contraindication lists in this catalog. All of it comes down to one thing: iodine. The alga is rich in it, and the monograph states that under the European Pharmacopoeia the iodine content of the powder must be between 0.03% and 0.2% — a numbered content requirement is rare in this catalog. The monograph’s sentence is plain: exceeding the daily iodine limit induces hyperthyroidism or worsens existing thyroid disease.

The North Atlantic

Fucus vesiculosus is a brown alga of the intertidal zone of the North Atlantic and North Sea coasts; the air bladders in its leafy thallus hold it upright in the water, which is where the Turkish name "kabarcıklı" (bladdered) comes from. It has no wild distribution on Turkish coasts.

In nature

Where it grows
The rocky intertidal zone of the North Atlantic and North Sea coasts. No wild distribution on Turkish coasts; imported as a product.
Harvest season
Present in the intertidal zone year-round; harvesting is usually done in spring and summer.
Local names
Esmer su yosunu, kabarcıklı alg, fukus — all three appear in the Turkish monograph.

Mix-up risk

⚠️ Two confusions. The species: products sold as "seaweed", "kelp" or "algae" may contain Laminaria, Ascophyllum or other species with different iodine contents, and the EU monograph covers Fucus vesiculosus only. The category: this is not a plant but a brown alga — a distinction herbal product labels often fail to make.

Preparation

The monograph describes preparations of the thallus. Here the standard of the preparation is the safety itself: the European Pharmacopoeia ties the iodine content of the powder to the range 0.03–0.2%. It must not be taken alongside other preparations containing Fucus, iodine-containing preparations, or medicines for the thyroid gland. Not recommended under 18.

What science says

The monograph’s indication is helping with weight loss, and there is a randomised trial that tests that indication directly. In a randomised, placebo-controlled, double-blind study published in 2022 in Marine Drugs, 56 overweight, dysglycaemic, insulin-resistant people took 500 mg a day of a brown seaweed extract (Ascophyllum nodosum and Fucus vesiculosus) or placebo for 12 weeks, both groups on individualised nutritional advice for moderate weight loss. The result is clear: the seaweed extract had no effect on body weight, and none on blood glucose either. The authors report an early attenuation of the inflammatory response and small changes in a few metabolic measures, but the study’s own title sums the finding up: the impact is marginal. Two notes are needed: the trial tested a mixture of two algae, not Fucus alone; and one trial is not a systematic review. The EU monograph rests its indication on long use. The evidence level is Traditional: a trial of the indication exists and found no effect on weight.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Thyroid medicines and iodine-containing productsBoth the EU and the Turkish monograph state it plainly: other preparations containing Fucus, iodine-containing preparations and medicines for the thyroid gland should not be taken alongside this alga. The reason is cumulative: each adds iodine, and exceeding the daily limit affects the thyroid.
LithiumThe EU monograph reports one thing in its interaction section, and it is a case report: hyperthyroidism published in a patient diagnosed with bipolar disorder and treated with lithium. Because lithium itself can affect thyroid function, this combination calls for particular care.
Heart and blood pressure medicinesNot a reported interaction, but the Turkish monograph says the alga is not to be used in cardiovascular and coronary disease. A change in thyroid function can affect heart rate and rhythm, so anyone on heart medication should ask their doctor about this product.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying Bladderwrack.
