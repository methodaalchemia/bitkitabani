Tea

Camellia sinensis
Evidence: LimitedGenerally safe

Tea took hold in the eastern Black Sea thanks to its acidic soil and rain; the first seedlings came to Rize from Georgia.

Other names
Green tea, black tea, çay (Turkish)
Part used
Leaves
Region
Asia and Siberia
Anatolia and the Mediterranean
Use
Food, Healing, Ritual and shamanic
In short

What is Tea?

Tea (Camellia sinensis) is a plant of the Theaceae family. Traditions that use it: Asia and Siberia, Anatolia and the Mediterranean. Part used: leaves.

What is Tea used for?

Traditional use: Drunk in China for thousands of years; the Japanese tea ceremony developed as a meditation practice. Scientific evidence: limited. Contains caffeine, L-theanine and catechins.

Is Tea safe?

Safety label: generally safe. Safe as a drink. High-dose green tea extract capsules have been linked to liver damage. Drinking it very hot is a risk for the oesophagus.

Traditional use

Asia

Drunk in China for thousands of years; the Japanese tea ceremony developed as a meditation practice.

Anatolia

Grown in Rize and the eastern Black Sea since the 1940s. Türkiye ranks at the top of the world in tea drunk per person; tea is the symbol of hospitality.

In nature

Where it grows
Rainy slopes of the eastern Black Sea; China, India and Sri Lanka.
Harvest season
Rize has three flushes between May and October.
Local names
çay, kara çay, filiz çay

Mix-up risk

Herbal teas such as chamomile or linden are called “tea” but are not Camellia sinensis and contain no caffeine.

Preparation

Leaves are steeped in boiled water; green tea at a lower temperature.

What science says

Contains caffeine, L-theanine and catechins. In observational studies regular tea drinking is linked to lower cardiovascular risk; the slimming effect of green tea extract is small.

Safety

Generally safe

Safe as a drink. High-dose green tea extract capsules have been linked to liver damage. Drinking it very hot is a risk for the oesophagus.
