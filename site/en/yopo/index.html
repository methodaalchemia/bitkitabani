In short

What is Yopo?

Yopo (Anadenanthera peregrina) is a plant of the Fabaceae family. Traditions that use it: Amazon, Andes. Part used: seeds.

What is Yopo used for?

Traditional use: Roasted seed powder is blown into the nose through a bone tube; shamans use it to contact spirits and to heal. Scientific evidence: traditional. Contains bufotenine, DMT and 5-MeO-DMT.

Is Yopo safe?

Safety label: high risk. Can cause vomiting, racing heart and high blood pressure. Dangerous for people with heart disease or a psychiatric history. Legal status: Restricted in Türkiye.

Traditional use

Amazon and Orinoco

Roasted seed powder is blown into the nose through a bone tube; shamans use it to contact spirits and to heal.

Andes

Its relative vilca appears with snuff kits in Andean archaeological finds at least 4,000 years old.

In nature

Where it grows
Open forests of the Orinoco basin, northern Amazon and Caribbean.
Harvest season
Seed pods are collected in the dry season.
Local names
No Turkish folk name. Local names: yopo, cohoba, paricá, vilca

Mix-up risk

Vilca (A. colubrina) and yopo are used similarly but differ chemically.

Preparation

Seeds are roasted, ground and mixed with lime to make snuff.

What science says

Contains bufotenine, DMT and 5-MeO-DMT. Effects are short and intense; there are no controlled human studies.

Safety

High risk

Can cause vomiting, racing heart and high blood pressure. Dangerous for people with heart disease or a psychiatric history.

Drug interactions

Medicine or substanceWhy it matters
Antidepressants and MAO inhibitorsRisk of serotonin syndrome with bufotenine and DMT.
Blood pressure and heart medicinesSudden changes in blood pressure and heart rate.

This list is not complete. If you take regular medication, ask your pharmacist or doctor before trying Yopo.

Legal status

Restricted in Türkiye

Its DMT and bufotenine are controlled substances.

Laws differ by country; check local rules before travelling with or buying any plant product.
