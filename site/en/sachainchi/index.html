What is Sacha inchi?

Sacha inchi (Plukenetia volubilis) is a plant of the Euphorbiaceae (spurge family) family. Traditions that use it: Amazon. Part used: seeds.

What is Sacha inchi used for?

Traditional use: In the Peruvian Amazon the roasted seeds are eaten and pressed for oil; some peoples rub the oil on the skin. Scientific evidence: limited. Rich in alpha-linolenic acid (omega-3) and protein.

Is Sacha inchi safe?

Safety label: caution. Raw seeds and leaves may contain toxic alkaloids; eat only roasted seeds.

Traditional use

Amazon

In the Peruvian Amazon the roasted seeds are eaten and pressed for oil; some peoples rub the oil on the skin.

Peru today

Exported worldwide as an omega-3 source.

In nature

Where it grows
Humid forest edges of the Peruvian Amazon; now grown in fields.
Harvest season
Fruit is picked all year when dry and brown.
Local names
No Turkish folk name. Local names: sacha inchi, sacha maní

Mix-up risk

Raw and roasted seeds look alike from the outside.

Preparation

The seeds must always be roasted before eating; raw seeds and leaves contain alkaloids and saponins, which roasting largely removes. The oil is extracted by cold pressing and is not used for frying: being rich in omega-3, it breaks down with heat, so it goes into salads and cold dishes.

What science says

Rich in alpha-linolenic acid (omega-3) and protein. A small study reported a slight improvement in cholesterol.

Safety

Caution

Raw seeds and leaves may contain toxic alkaloids; eat only roasted seeds.
